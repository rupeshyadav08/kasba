Cancer treatment is expensive, and medicines are a large part of the cost. To bring this down, the National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority (NPPA), in its order of 17 October 2026, capped the trade margin on 137 anti-cancer drugs at 30% of the maximum retail price (MRP). The new prices take effect from 9 November 2026.

## What has changed

Trade margin is the gap between the price a distributor pays the company and the price the patient pays (excluding GST). A large gap makes a medicine costly. Under the new order, each drug's new MRP is worked out on its average distributor price of March 2026, with a margin of no more than 30%, and GST added on top. The rule applies to all forms, including tablets and injections.

## How much prices have fallen

By 4 November 2026, 70 companies had reported revised prices for 120 drugs. MRP has fallen on average by 20% to 70%, with the highest cut at 96.45%. The estimated saving is Rs 3,357.49 crore. Medicines already cheaper than the new limit cannot be made more expensive.

## What companies and shops must do

Companies must inform NPPA of revised prices, publish the price cuts in newspapers, display them on their websites and share them with all retailers and hospitals. They cannot reduce production without permission and must give 6 months' notice before stopping production of a drug entirely. Hospitals and medicine shops must display the new prices clearly. A company that does not cut prices must refund the excess amount charged, with interest.

## What patients can do

Patients can verify prices on the NPPA website and the official 'Pharma Sahi Daam' mobile app. Check the bill and the displayed price at the shop.

## Why this step was taken

The government regulates medicine prices under the Drugs (Prices Control) Order (DPCO), 2013. This order used the special public-interest powers under paragraph 19 of DPCO, 2013, on the recommendation of an expert committee under the Directorate General of Health Services. A similar pilot in 2019 covering 42 non-scheduled cancer drugs had successfully lowered costs for patients.
