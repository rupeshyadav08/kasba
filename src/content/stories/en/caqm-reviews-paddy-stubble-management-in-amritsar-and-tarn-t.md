## What happened

A high-level team led by Rajesh Verma, Chairperson of the Commission for Air Quality Management in NCR and adjoining areas (CAQM), held a review meeting with the Deputy Commissioners and other officials of Punjab's Amritsar and Tarn Taran districts on 24 October 2026. The meeting reviewed the steps being taken for paddy stubble management and stressed tighter control and complete elimination of stubble-burning incidents.

## What was reviewed

The team reviewed both in-field and off-field paddy stubble management in the two districts, along with the facilities and infrastructure available for it. It also reviewed the action taken by both districts in view of rising fire incidents. The team expressed concern over the increase in such incidents and directed officials to make every possible effort to prevent them in future. The Commission also directed that nodal officers be appointed for different sectors, responsible for ensuring actual use of paddy stubble in the concerned areas and industries and for reporting compliance through regular monitoring.

## Who benefits

The Commission stressed that paddy stubble should be used to the maximum as fuel in thermal power plants, brick kilns and industries where such use is possible. This can put stubble to productive use and reduce the need for farmers to burn it.

## Directions to officials

The Commission told officials to visit villages and farmers regularly. They should not only inform farmers about available stubble management facilities but also ensure that the necessary machinery and facilities reach farmers on time. District administrations have been asked to strengthen coordination among farmers, Custom Hiring Centres (CHCs), machinery providers and other stakeholders so that farmers can use residue management solutions without delay.

## Focus on Tarn Taran

According to the release, Tarn Taran district has reported the highest number of paddy stubble-burning incidents. Its Deputy Commissioner was directed to treat the issue as the top priority and step up ground-level efforts to prevent and manage such incidents. The Commission warned that any negligence or failure to take effective steps will invite strict action and responsibility will be fixed.

## What the team saw in Amritsar

During the visit, the team inspected facilities in Amritsar where paddy stubble is used as fuel. It visited an industrial boiler at an industrial complex that uses pellets made from paddy stubble as fuel. The visit offered a chance to understand the practical use of paddy residue and the scope for scaling up such useful models.

## What next

The Commission reiterated that paddy stubble management should be seen from the point of view of farmers' convenience, as part of a wider process of residue management and resource use. It stressed that district administrations and all stakeholders should work together so that available in-field and off-field solutions are implemented effectively, stubble is put to productive use and stubble-burning incidents are eliminated completely.
