## What the fair is and where it is held

The Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment has organised a 15-day Shilp Samagam Mela 2026 at Delhi Haat in INA, Delhi. The fair runs till 15 November 2026. The ministry said its theme is 'Prakash aur Parampara – Jyoti se Jyoti Jalayein' (light and tradition).

The fair displays handicrafts, handloom products, traditional textiles, festive decorations, lamps, home decor and other indigenous products. These are spread across more than 130 stalls in 7 themed zones. The zones are named Kala Aangan, Prakash Chowk, Puja Path, Sajawat Bazar, Gharana, Swad Chowk and Manch.

## What the sales figures show

According to a senior official of the ministry, total sales of about Rs 55.46 lakh were recorded between 1 and 8 November 2026. The sales came from stalls of four corporations — the National Scheduled Castes Finance and Development Corporation (NSFDC), the National Backward Classes Finance and Development Corporation (NBCFDC), the National Safai Karamcharis Finance and Development Corporation (NSKDFC) and the National Divyangjan Finance and Development Corporation (NDFDC).

The ministry says these figures show that visitors are responding well to the products and that market opportunities for artisans are growing.

## Who benefits

Artisans and beneficiaries linked to Scheduled Caste, Other Backward Class, sanitation worker and disabled communities can display and sell their products directly to a large audience at the fair. The ministry provides them free stalls, travel expenses, a daily allowance and transport of their goods, so that cost and logistics do not come in the way of their participation.

The ministry has been holding Shilp Samagam fairs since 2022. Through these fairs, more than 1,000 artisans from Scheduled Caste, Other Backward Class and sanitation worker communities have so far got a chance to display and sell their products. The aim is to promote self-employment and support the economic empowerment of these communities.

## What it means for visitors

The fair is open to the public. Families and young visitors can come to see traditional products and buy directly from artisans. Every evening, cultural programmes are held at the 'Manch' stage, where artists and cultural troupes from different states present folk dances, traditional music and regional art forms.

The ministry also said that initiatives such as Bharat TULIP connect artisans to e-commerce platforms, and initiatives such as Purple Fest celebrate the creativity and abilities of persons with disabilities.
