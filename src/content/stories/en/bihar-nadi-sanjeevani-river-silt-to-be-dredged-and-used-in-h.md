## What has happened

The Ministry of Road Transport and Highways and the Bihar government signed a memorandum of understanding in New Delhi on 3 November 2026. Under it, silt will be removed scientifically from identified rivers and other water bodies in Bihar, and that silt will be used in national highway projects. The initiative is called 'Nadi Sanjeevani'. The signing took place in the presence of Union Minister for Road Transport and Highways Nitin Gadkari and Bihar Chief Minister Samrat Chaudhary.

## Why this is being done

According to the notice, large amounts of silt collecting in rivers have hurt their water-holding capacity, adding to problems such as floods and damage to farmland and property. Bihar faces repeated floods and water stress, and rivers coming from Nepal bring heavy water and silt into the state. The notice says removing silt will improve water flow and drainage, revive water bodies and also provide material for highway construction.

## Silt use in highway projects

The National Highways Authority of India (NHAI) has identified wide scope for using the silt in highway projects in Bihar. As per the notice, 243 lakh cubic metres of suitable silt will be used in 16 ongoing projects, while about 835 lakh cubic metres is proposed for use in 27 future projects. The silt will be used subject to technical suitability and required approvals.

At present, 39 NHAI projects of about 1,561 km are under construction in Bihar, with about Rs 58,340 crore of investment stated. Another 11 projects of about 1,982 km are proposed, involving about Rs 1,85,200 crore of investment.

## How the work will be done and who monitors it

Bihar's Road Construction Department will act as the nodal department. A state-level coordination committee will examine proposals, facilitate approvals, monitor implementation and resolve operational issues. Silt removal will be taken up only after a detailed technical assessment of the identified water bodies, and permission will be given only at approved sites within set technical limits. It will also be ensured that silt removal does not harm the river ecosystem, riverbanks, embankments, bridges and other structures.

## State policy and relief for farmers

The Chief Minister said the 'Bihar Water Source Revival and Silt Management Policy, 2026' has been prepared to provide a structured framework for reviving water bodies and managing silt. According to the notice, the policy also provides for removing sand and silt left on farmland after floods, which will give relief to affected farmers.

## What this means for ordinary people

The agreement gives the public no application, form or deadline, so nobody needs to do anything. The benefits described in the notice — lower flood risk, more water for irrigation and drinking, and protection of farmland — are long-term. For more information, rely on the concerned department or official government information.
