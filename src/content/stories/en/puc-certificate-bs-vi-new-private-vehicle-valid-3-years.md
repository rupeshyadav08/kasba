## What has changed

The Ministry of Road Transport and Highways has proposed an amendment to sub-rule (7) of Central Motor Vehicles Rule 115. Under it, the validity of a pollution control (PUC) certificate will depend on the vehicle's emission standard and its age. The ministry says the aim is to set how often emission tests are needed as a vehicle ages and to keep continuous watch on vehicle emissions.

## Validity by vehicle type

As proposed, PUC certificates for BS-I, BS-II and BS-III vehicles will be valid for 3 months. For BS-IV vehicles the proposed period is 6 months.

BS-VI vehicles have a separate schedule. For a private (non-transport) BS-VI vehicle, a PUC is needed once every 3 years up to 6 years from first registration. From more than 6 years up to 10 years it is once a year, and beyond 10 years once every 6 months. For BS-VI transport vehicles, the proposed period is every 2 years up to 6 years, every year from 6 to 10 years, and every 6 months beyond 10 years.

## What about existing certificates

Certificates issued before the amendment takes effect will get the revised validity from their first renewal after it comes into force. In other words, old certificates run for their stated period, and the new validity applies when they are next renewed.

## Mind the renewal window

The ministry has also set renewal rules. If a PUC is renewed within 10 days before the current certificate expires, the new certificate is valid from the old one's expiry date. If it is renewed after expiry, the new certificate counts from the date of renewal.

## Who benefits

According to the ministry, the proposal reduces the compliance burden on owners of new and low-polluting vehicles, who will not need a PUC as often. Older vehicles will still be checked at fixed intervals.

## What to do next

The notice does not state a date for when the amendment takes effect. When getting a PUC made or renewed, check your vehicle's emission standard (BS-IV or BS-VI) and its registration date. For confirmation of when the rule applies, check the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways release or ask your local transport office.
