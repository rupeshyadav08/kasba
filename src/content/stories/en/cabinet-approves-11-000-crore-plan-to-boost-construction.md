## What has happened

The Union Cabinet on 25 November 2026 approved a scheme with a financial outlay of ₹11,000 crore to promote domestic manufacturing of construction and infrastructure equipment (CIE). The Ministry of Heavy Industries will implement it. CIE refers to the large machines used to build roads, bridges, ports and buildings.

## What the scheme contains

It has two parts. First, a sales-linked incentive: approved applicants under two categories — eligible OEMs and component manufacturers — will get incentives on the incremental sales value of eligible CIE equipment and components, at graded rates from 13% down to 8% over five years. Second, a grant of up to ₹375 crore as viability gap funding to set up a dedicated CIE testing facility centre in the country.

The scheme runs for eight years starting from financial year 2026-27. The first two years (2026-27 and 2027-28) are a pre-production period for setting up manufacturing units. Then come five years of incentive disbursement (2028-29 to 2032-33), and a final year (2033-34) to settle incentives based on incremental sales in 2032-33.

## Which machines are the focus

The scheme focuses on advanced-technology equipment such as ship-to-shore cranes, tunnel boring machines, hydraulic excavators and off-highway trucks. The aim is to have these made in India and to secure the supply chain.

## Why the government says it matters

According to the release, India's CIE industry is the world's third largest after China and the US. Its market size was about ₹97,500 crore in FY 2024-25, and with fast growth in roads, ports and housing it is expected to reach about ₹2,28,100 crore by FY 2029-30.

## What it means for ordinary people

There is nothing for an ordinary citizen to apply for, and no individual gets money under it. It is only for companies that make this equipment or its components. The release says the scheme will help increase employment opportunities.
