## What happened

The National Biodiversity Authority (NBA) has distributed ₹13.76 crore across 28 states and 4 union territories to promote biodiversity conservation and the sustainable use of biological resources. The money is part of contributions received from companies that make AYUSH products and commercially use medicinal plants and other plant-based biological resources.

## What the ABS mechanism is

ABS stands for Access and Benefit Sharing. In simple terms, when a company commercially uses medicinal plants sourced from forests or fields, a share of the benefit is paid towards biodiversity conservation. This money is routed through state biodiversity boards and used to support conservation, sustainable harvesting and cultivation of these plants.

## Where the contributions came from

The contributions came under three applications covering the use of 234 plant species. These resources were purchased through markets and trader networks, which made it difficult to identify individual benefit claimants. However, since most medicinal plants used by companies come from forest areas, the forest cover of the state or union territory and the availability of medicinal plants there are taken into account while distributing the money.

## Which plants contributed the most

Of the 234 species, ten — guggul, ashwagandha, bhringraj, punarnava, sarpagandha, kalmegh, black pepper, amla, gokhru and shalparni — account for ₹6.45 crore of the total ₹13.76 crore.

## How much each state got

Maharashtra received the most at ₹1,75,24,129, followed by Arunachal Pradesh at ₹93,74,889, Andhra Pradesh at ₹88,07,925, Karnataka at ₹87,14,794 and Kerala at ₹79,30,040. Madhya Pradesh received ₹69,90,463, Tamil Nadu ₹63,03,300, Jharkhand ₹56,77,312, Rajasthan ₹52,75,096, Chhattisgarh ₹52,21,105, Gujarat ₹48,16,750, Assam ₹46,83,668, West Bengal ₹42,65,005 and Himachal Pradesh ₹40,81,731. The 'others' category received ₹3,00,80,041.

## What the money is used for

The funds can support updating people's biodiversity registers, documenting biological resources and related traditional knowledge, research and capacity building, community-based conservation, restoration of degraded ecosystems, sustainable harvesting and farming practices, and biodiversity-based livelihood initiatives.

## The running total

NBA has so far collected ₹278 crore through the ABS mechanism, of which about ₹218.86 crore has been distributed for conservation and benefit-sharing initiatives across the country. The mechanism advances Target 13 of the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework (KMGBF), which deals with the fair and equitable sharing of benefits from genetic resources.

## What it means for ordinary people

The money does not go directly into any individual's account, so citizens do not need to apply. But communities involved in collecting, farming, processing or trading medicinal plants may benefit from conservation and livelihood initiatives in their area. These efforts complement government schemes such as the National AYUSH Mission, National Medicinal Plants Board programmes and the Pradhan Mantri Van Dhan Yojana.
