New prices for 137 medicines used in cancer treatment came into force on 9 November 2026. The National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority (NPPA), which comes under the Department of Pharmaceuticals in the Ministry of Chemicals and Fertilizers, has capped the trade margin on the maximum retail price (MRP) of these drugs.

## What has changed

NPPA has fixed a limit on the trade margin on the MRP of 137 anti-cancer drugs, which led to their revised prices. The step is based on the recommendations of an expert committee set up by the Directorate General of Health Services (DGHS) under the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare on 17 October 2026.

## How many drugs became cheaper

By 8 November 2026, 85 pharmaceutical companies had reported revised MRP for 1,364 anti-cancer formulations linked to 121 drugs. Of these, 679 brands cut prices by more than 50 per cent. The highest cut in MRP was recorded at 96.45 per cent.

## Who benefits

The price cuts are estimated to save consumers about Rs 3,540.38 crore a year. In other words, cancer patients and their families will spend less on these medicines.

## What you should do

When buying a medicine, check the revised MRP printed on the pack. You can also confirm the price on the 'Pharma Sahi Daam' mobile app or on NPPA's website (nppa.gov.in). No chemist or hospital pharmacy can charge more than the revised MRP.

## Where to complain

If a shop charges more than the revised MRP or you have any other price-related problem, you can file a complaint on the 'Pharma Jan Samadhan' portal. You can also contact the NPPA helpline 1800-111-255.
