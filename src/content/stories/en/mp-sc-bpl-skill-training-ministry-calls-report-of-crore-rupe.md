## What happened

The Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment issued a fact-check report on 1 November 2026. The report responds to a news story published in the Central Chronicle (Bhopal) on 31 October 2026. That story claimed the Madhya Pradesh Employment and Training Council (MAPCET) paid crores on the basis of placement without verifying placement data. The Ministry has called the claim completely false and baseless.

## Where the money came from

According to the report, the office of the Commissioner, Scheduled Caste Development Department, sanctioned Rs 3,241.37 lakh for skill development training under the "Special Central Assistance to the Scheduled Caste Sub-Plan" head. Of this, Rs 2,945.37 lakh was made available to MAPCET during 2021-22. This included Rs 1,296.00 lakh through order number 4695 dated 26 November 2021 and Rs 1,649.37 lakh through order number 10283 dated 25 March 2022.

## Who was trained

MAPCET issued a Request for Proposal (RFP) for 2021-22 to train beneficiaries from Scheduled Caste families living below the poverty line (BPL). After selection, contracts were signed with 34 training provider organisations and work orders were issued. Six of these organisations did not start work. The remaining 28 institutions trained 4,952 trainees.

## What the payment rules say

Under notification number 349 issued by the Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship on 4 December 2020, training providers running batch-based training are paid in three instalments. The first instalment is 30 per cent at the start of training, the second 40 per cent on successful completion of certification, and the third 30 per cent on achieving the target of 70 per cent verified employment. The third instalment requires three months of continuous employment with desk verification and placement verification as per standard norms.

## Why the third instalment was not paid

The report says the first instalment was paid when the batch started, after physical verification of trainees, and the second after training was completed and trainees assessed. Assessment is done through agencies nominated by the National Council for Vocational Education and Training (NCVET), and at least 70 per cent attendance is required to be eligible. Institutions submitted placement information in hard copy based on job offers or offer letters; this does not form the basis for the third instalment. To be eligible, institutions must upload placement details on the portal, and only then can the portal generate a payment claim. Since no such claim was generated for any institution, no third instalment was paid.

## What happens next

The report says the third instalment will be paid when trainees complete training and get employment. At present, the organisations are helping trainees get placements.
