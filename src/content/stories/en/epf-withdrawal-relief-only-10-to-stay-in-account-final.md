## What was decided

The Central Board of Trustees (CBT) of the Employees' Provident Fund met for its 240th meeting in New Delhi on 19 November 2026, chaired by Union Labour and Employment Minister Dr Mansukh Mandaviya. EPFO placed several proposals before the board, which were approved. These include easier withdrawal rules and a scheme to take retirement savings in instalments.

## What changed in withdrawal rules

Until now, a member had to keep 25% of the contributions in the account as a minimum balance at all times. This has been cut to 10%. The waiting period for a final withdrawal has also been reduced from 12 months to 2 months. According to the notice, the aim is to give members more financial flexibility while protecting their long-term social security interests.

## What the systematic withdrawal scheme is

The board approved a proposal to start a systematic withdrawal scheme. Under it, retiring members can receive their accumulated PF money in regular instalments instead of withdrawing the whole amount at once. Members can choose monthly, quarterly or half-yearly payouts. The remaining deposit keeps earning returns, which helps add to income after retirement.

The facility is fully voluntary — no member has to withdraw under it. The notice says the option may also be extended to eligible family members or beneficiaries. In the first phase, about 6.32 lakh EPF members above 55 years of age with more than Rs 5 lakh in their fund are expected to be the main beneficiaries. If a member dies during the payout period, the nominee or dependent can withdraw the remaining amount or continue receiving regular payments.

## Decisions on EDLI and investments

The board approved amendments to the EDLI Scheme, 2026. Eligible establishments will get six months to renew lapsed exemptions, and relief from contributions, interest and penal damages for a verified transitional period. The board also gave in-principle approval to invest up to 3% of new inflows in eligible alternative investment funds.

## The CITES 2.0 effect

The board was told that after CITES 2.0, a central digital platform developed by C-DAC, went live, the share of claims settled automatically rose from 39% to 71%. The overall claim rejection rate fell from 23% to 7.82%, and the average settlement time came down from 11.15 days to 2.57 days.

## What happens next

According to the notice, necessary amendments to the EPF Scheme will be notified after the Ministry's approval. A proposal to amend the Income Tax Act in this connection is being placed before the Finance Ministry. The notice does not give an effective date.
