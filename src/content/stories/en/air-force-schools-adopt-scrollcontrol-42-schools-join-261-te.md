## What has happened

Under Air Headquarters, the Indian Air Force Educational and Cultural Society has linked Air Force schools across the country to the child digital safety programme 'ScrollControl' through the Information Sharing and Analysis Centre (ISAC) Foundation. The initiative was launched alongside National Cyber Security Awareness Month.

According to the notice, 42 Air Force schools are part of the programme, and 261 teachers from these schools have completed a certification course as cyber crime intervention officers.

## Why this was done

The notice says affordable high-speed internet, smart devices and easy access to social media mean schoolchildren are increasingly coming into contact with digital spaces designed mainly for adults.

That concern prompted the move. The training aims to protect children from online enticement and exploitation, misuse of artificial intelligence, risks linked to online gaming and financial fraud.

## What training teachers received

According to the notice, teachers have also been trained to preserve digital evidence. In the event of a cyber crime, they can act as first responders.

The notice also says the Central Board of Secondary Education (CBSE) has directed its affiliated schools to observe a 'Cyber Awareness Day' every month. This initiative will help Air Force schools build a trained internal system of their own.

## What schools will get

Under the programme, Air Force schools can set up cyber clubs. They can give students certified training in cyber safety and hold awareness sessions for parents and staff.

Families will also get access to a platform that rates digital content by age. Schools can also be recognised as a 'Cyber Safe School'.

## Support for victims

According to the notice, along with training and certification of teachers, the ISAC Foundation is arranging to panel them in the national security database. A multi-disciplinary mechanism of cyber advocates, cyber psychologists and forensic experts is also being set up to give victims 24-hour support.

## What this means for ordinary people

The programme is for Air Force schools, so ordinary parents have no application to make. Families whose children study in these schools can ask the school about its cyber club and awareness sessions.
