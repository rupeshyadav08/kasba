## What has been announced

The Higher Education Department of the Ministry of Education has announced the fifth edition of Kashi Tamil Sangamam. It will run from 28 November to 12 December 2026 on the theme "Tirukkural Paailavom – Let us learn Tirukkural". The opening is on 28 November and the closing ceremony on 12 December 2026.

The event is organised with IIT Madras, Banaras Hindu University, the Central Institute of Classical Tamil (CICT) Chennai and the Uttar Pradesh government. Several central ministries, including Culture, Railways, Tourism and Textiles, are partners.

## What is new this time

As before, about 1,400 participants from Tamil Nadu will visit Varanasi, Prayagraj and Ayodhya. They come from seven categories: students, teachers, writers and media professionals, agriculture and allied fields, professionals and artisans, women, and spiritual gurus.

The new element is that Tirukkural will be taken to educational institutions across the country. A national student engagement programme will run through 1,000 higher education institutions. Fifty teachers from Tamil Nadu will also come to Uttar Pradesh to teach Tirukkural in schools and colleges.

## What students get

Each partner institution will evaluate its interested students on Tirukkural and nominate one student on merit. Selected students will attend an online academic orientation in 22 scheduled languages and English. This will be followed by a month of research and academic engagement, with multilingual study material from CICT.

Each selected student will get a ₹10,000 stipend, subject to meeting the programme guidelines and set requirements. The student must write a 4 to 10 page article or research note under a faculty guide and give a one-hour presentation on Tirukkural at the institution. Selected students will join both the opening and closing events online.

## What is Tirukkural

Tirukkural is a classical Tamil text seen as a symbol of ethics and conduct. Its author Tiruvalluvar divided it into three parts: Aram (virtue), Porul (wealth) and Inbam (love). The text has 1,330 couplets, and CICT has translated it into 25 Indian languages, including all 22 languages of the Eighth Schedule of the Constitution, along with several foreign languages and Braille.

## What happens in Varanasi

Participants will visit major religious, historical and cultural sites including the Kashi Vishwanath temple, Sarnath or BHU, Ramnagar Fort and Bharat Mata Mandir. The itinerary also includes Ganga Aarti, a Ganga dip, academic sessions and cultural programmes. A special Tamil heritage circuit will take participants to the ancestral home of Mahakavi Subramania Bharati, Kedar Ghat and Little Tamil Nadu.

BHU will host a national conference on Tirukkural. The Ministry of Culture will provide cultural troupes, screenings of short films on Tirukkural and cultural performances. Handicraft and handloom outlets from Tamil Nadu and Uttar Pradesh will be set up at the main venue. The National Book Trust will hold interactive sessions on reading habits, storytelling and creative activities in Varanasi schools.

## What it means for ordinary readers

There is nothing for the general public to apply for, and no last date has been announced. Students should ask their institution whether it is taking part. CICT's multilingual Tirukkural app offers all 1,330 couplets with translations in 22 scheduled languages and is open to anyone. Signboards with translations of couplets are also planned at railway stations, airports and highway toll plazas.
