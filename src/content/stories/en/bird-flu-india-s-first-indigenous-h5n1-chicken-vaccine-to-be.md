## What has happened

Union Minister for Agriculture and Farmers' Welfare Shivraj Singh Chouhan will dedicate a vaccine for chickens to the nation on 20 November 2026 at ICAR-National Institute of High Security Animal Diseases (ICAR-NIHSAD) in Bhopal. It is an inactivated H5N1 (clade 2.3.2.1a) vaccine against the highly pathogenic avian influenza (HPAI) virus.

According to the notice, this is the first vaccine of its kind developed in India against the H5N1 virus clade 2.3.2.1a. It has been developed indigenously by ICAR-NIHSAD, Bhopal.

## What bird flu is and why it worries farmers

Highly pathogenic avian influenza, commonly called bird flu, is a serious disease of chickens and other birds. The notice says up to about 100 percent of infected birds can die in an outbreak. Large-scale killing of birds and trade restrictions cause heavy financial losses to poultry farmers.

The impact does not stay limited to farms. It also affects trade, the prices of poultry products and farmers' livelihoods. The notice says that since the disease was first detected in India in 2006, more than 500 combined outbreaks of the H5N1 and H5N8 subtypes have been recorded in the country.

## How big the losses can be

According to the notice, a major bird flu outbreak can cut India's GDP growth by up to 1.5 percent. If the disease spreads unchecked, more than 20 percent of the country's poultry population is at risk of being affected and destroyed.

The 2006 outbreak caused an estimated direct and indirect loss of Rs 2,200 to 3,000 crore. An outbreak in Manipur caused a loss equal to 0.5 percent of the state's GDP.

## Concern for human health

The disease is also a public health concern. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), 1,003 human cases of H5N1 avian influenza were reported in 25 countries, including India, from 2003 onwards, with 479 deaths.

## Why the vaccine was needed

Studies of the virus showed that the form called clade 2.3.2.1a was responsible for most bird flu outbreaks in chickens. Recently, infections from this virus have been found in various animal species, including mammals. Three human cases of this infection have also been reported.

The notice says no specific commercial vaccine is currently available to protect against this clade. ICAR-NIHSAD developed this vaccine to meet that need, with the aim of protecting against the H5N1 clade 2.3.2.1a virus circulating in the country.

## What farmers should do

The notice does not give any date, method or condition for getting the vaccine. For now, poultry farmers should keep their farms clean and keep watching for signs of disease. For further information on the vaccine, they can contact their nearest veterinary office or animal husbandry department.
