## What is CCUS

CCUS — carbon capture, utilisation and storage — is a system in which carbon dioxide from factories or power plants is caught before it goes into the air. The gas is then cleaned, compressed and sent onward. The captured gas can be used for a useful purpose or kept safely underground.

## Why it is being pushed

India's target is net zero emissions by 2070. Renewable energy can cut emissions a great deal, but some industries cannot easily run on electricity because they need very high temperatures or carbon-based inputs. NITI Aayog has called CCUS important for hard-to-abate sectors such as steel, cement, oil and gas, petrochemicals, chemicals and fertiliser.

## How the process works

In the first step, carbon dioxide is captured at the plant and compressed into a clean, dry, dense form. In the second, it is usually moved by pipeline; ships and other modes can offer flexibility, especially over long distances. In the third step, the gas is either used or stored permanently underground.

## Where it is used and stored

Captured carbon dioxide can be used in enhanced oil recovery, food and beverages, urea, green methanol and concrete. For permanent storage, it is injected deep into geological structures such as depleted oil and gas reservoirs, deep saline aquifers and basaltic rock formations.

## Hub and cluster model

India's CCUS framework envisages regional clusters and hubs that link several emitters through shared infrastructure. This can allow shared systems for transport, use and storage. The DST's CCUS Roadmap 2025 identifies possible regions such as the Gujarat coast, the Odisha–Jharkhand belt and the Northeast.

## Government initiatives

The Union Budget 2026-27 announced a CCUS plan of Rs 20,000 crore over five years for power, steel, cement, refineries and chemicals. India's first integrated CCUS field laboratory opened at IIT Bombay in May 2026 and can capture up to 3 tonnes of carbon dioxide a day. Separately, the ESTIC-2026 conference is being held at Bharat Mandapam, New Delhi, from 27 to 29 October 2026, where emerging technologies including CCUS will be discussed.

## What it means for ordinary people

This matter does not directly involve ordinary consumers. There is no application to file and no deadline to meet. The information is mainly relevant to people in industry, research and the environment sector.
