## What happened

The Department of Financial Services (DFS) in the Finance Ministry has released the results of Special Campaign 6. The department ran the campaign across the country with its attached organisations, regional offices and autonomous bodies. The stated aims were to make cleanliness part of the institutional work culture, free up valuable office space, beautify office premises and dispose of scrap and e-waste properly.

The DFS Secretary visited different sections of the department to review the activities, cleanliness drives and space management steps taken under the campaign.

## Which organisations were involved

The department worked closely with public sector banks (PSBs), public sector insurance companies (PSICs), public sector financial institutions (PSFIs), regulators, regional rural banks (RRBs) and Debt Recovery Tribunals and Debt Recovery Appellate Tribunals (DRT/DRAT). These are the bodies that handle people's banking, insurance and loan-related services.

## What the campaign achieved

According to the release, 1,20,750 cleanliness sites, drives or offices were cleaned, which is 134 per cent of the set target. Office space of 6,99,201 square feet was freed up. Scrap disposal earned revenue of Rs 4,12,58,987.

Separately, 31,327 public grievances were settled, along with 3,564 appeals related to public grievances.

## Waste-to-art and cyber safety

Several public sector banks turned unused plastic, old hardware and faulty electronic items into benches, chairs, trays, dustbins and decorative office sculptures under 'Waste to Art' activities. These were placed inside and outside offices.

To spread awareness on cyber security, public sector banks and RRBs shared videos and posts on social media. These posts carried safety tips and steps to avoid cyber crime and fraud. During the campaign, the State Bank of India also released an e-booklet on cyber security.

## What it means for ordinary people

This is a report on the internal working of government financial organisations, so citizens do not have to fill any form or apply for anything. For those who had filed a complaint with a public sector bank, insurance company or RRB, it is useful to know that grievance disposal was part of the campaign. The department said it is committed to making better office facilities, digital safety and faster delivery of public services a permanent part of its work culture.
