## What has started

According to the Ministry of Home Affairs, self-enumeration (SE) for the second phase of Census 2027, the Population Enumeration (PE), began on 16 November 2026 in the non-snow-bound areas of Goa, Uttar Pradesh, Punjab and Uttarakhand. Residents of these states can submit their household details online till 30 November 2026.

## How to self-enumerate

Residents can log in at the official portal se.census.gov.in using their mobile number; the notice says preference is given to the number registered during the first phase. On the portal, they answer 40 notified questions on household and individual details. Once the details are submitted successfully, a unique Self-Enumeration ID (SE ID) is generated, which must be shared with the enumerator when they visit to complete the process. The facility is available in 16 Indian languages.

## When house-to-house counting happens

In these states and areas, house-to-house population enumeration will be carried out from 1 December 2026 to 4 January 2027. A revision round will follow from 5 to 9 January 2027. The reference date for these states is 5 January 2027 at 00:00 hours.

## What happens if you do not self-enumerate

Enumerators will continue house-to-house surveys in all allotted blocks during their field visits. Households that do not choose self-enumeration will be covered through the enumerators' regular door-to-door visits.

## What about the other states

In the remaining states and Union Territories, population enumeration will be held from 25 January to 28 February 2027, followed by a revision round from 1 to 5 March 2027. Before enumerators begin their visits there, self-enumeration will be available from 10 to 24 January 2027. The reference date for these states and UTs is 1 March 2027 at 00:00 hours.

## What happened in the first phase

Under the first phase of Census 2027, the Houselisting and Housing Census (HLO) has been completed in 35 states and Union Territories, excluding Manipur. The notice says Census 2027 is being conducted using digital tools for the first time, with self-enumeration added as an additional facility.

## Privacy of information

All information collected under the Census Act, 1948 remains fully confidential. It is used only for statistical purposes and development planning. Residents have been encouraged to take part in this national exercise and cooperate with enumerators.
