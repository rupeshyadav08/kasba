## What was decided

The Union Cabinet, chaired by the Prime Minister, on 25 November 2026 approved continuing the central sector scheme "Dak Operational Plan". It will run during the 16th Finance Commission cycle, from financial year 2026-27 to 2030-31, with a proposed outlay of ₹1,141 crore.

The plan aims to strengthen postal infrastructure, improve operational efficiency and meet the growing demand for logistics, e-commerce and digital service delivery.

## Who benefits

India Post runs one of the world's largest postal networks, with 1.65 lakh post offices in all, including 1,49,655 in rural areas. The plan focuses on stronger last-mile delivery, financial inclusion and better citizen-centric services, especially in remote and underserved areas.

Post offices already offer more than mail and parcels: Post Office Savings Bank (POSB), India Post Payments Bank (IPPB), Postal Life Insurance (PLI), Aadhaar enrolment and update, passport services through post office passport service centres, eKYC verification, and support to rural artisans through Dak Niryat Kendras.

## The six parts of the plan

- **Rural business and postal network access — ₹120 crore:** upgrading infrastructure, standardised branding and better use of resources so rural post offices work as efficient service centres.
- **Optimising the postal network — ₹575 crore:** centralised delivery systems, mechanisation, advanced IT solutions, GIS-based services, stronger delivery post offices, mail management systems, Mail Motor Service (MMS) workshops, road transport network and smart 24x7 letter boxes.
- **Optimising the parcel network — ₹278 crore:** building parcel hubs, expanding parcel packaging units (PPUs) and redesigning sorting to cut handling points and transit time.
- **International mail — ₹8 crore:** upgrading Foreign Post Offices (FPOs) and Sub Foreign Post Offices (SFPOs) to speed up processing of international mail.
- **Marketing activities, including service quality — ₹100 crore:** multi-media campaigns on TV, digital, radio, print and outdoor, social media campaigns and Dak Chaupal activities, especially in rural areas.
- **Stamp collecting activities — ₹60 crore:** exhibitions, seminars, quizzes, school stamp clubs, innovative stamps such as scented, holographic and 3D stamps, the 'Dhai Aakhar' letter-writing competition and the 'Sparsh' scholarship.

## What ordinary people need to do

The decision gives the public no application, form or last date. It is approval of government spending, so nobody has to act right now. The stated aim is better postal services, and any change would show up over time in how post offices work.
