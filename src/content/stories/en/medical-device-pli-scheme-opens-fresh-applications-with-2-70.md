## What has happened

The Department of Pharmaceuticals has invited fresh applications under the production-linked incentive scheme for medical devices. The scheme has now been extended to 2035-36 and has an outlay of about ₹2,700 crore. Applications will be made through an online portal that will start in the next 10 days.

## What the scheme is

The scheme began in 2021-22. Its aim is to boost domestic manufacturing of medical devices so that more of them are made in India. So far, domestic manufacturing of more than 60 high-end medical devices and their components has begun under the scheme. These include X-ray tubes and detectors, 1.5 Tesla MRI, linac, CT scanners, mammography machines, cathlab, bone and cardiac implants, and dialysers. Devices worth more than ₹16,000 crore have been produced under the scheme so far.

## What has changed

Based on the experience of the first phase, eligibility conditions and the process for claiming incentives have been simplified. The minimum sales condition for getting incentives has been removed and the minimum investment requirement has been reduced. Applicants can apply in one or more segments, and the application process will remain open for a long time.

## What are the four segments

Applicants must set up a unit in one of four target segments and will get three years for it. TS-1 covers medical consumables, disposable products and implants. TS-2 covers critical components. TS-3 covers high-end devices whose domestic manufacturing is still at an early stage, and TS-4 covers high-end devices that are currently only assembled in India.

## What is the incentive rate

The tapering average incentive rate is about 5% for TS-1, about 9% for TS-2 and TS-3, and 6% for TS-4.

## Who benefits

The scheme directly benefits manufacturers and investors who want to set up medical device units. The government says this will increase domestic manufacturing of critical components and also support the MSME sector. The scheme aims to encourage manufacturing of devices such as 3 Tesla MRI, PET-CT, ultrasound probes and consoles, and high-capacity IVD analysers, which are not made in India yet.

## What it means for ordinary people

Ordinary citizens do not have to apply for anything under this scheme. The stated aim is to improve the quality of India-made medical devices and bring down their prices.
