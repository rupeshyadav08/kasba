## What happened

Union Health and Family Welfare Minister Jagat Prakash Nadda spoke about the country's progress after the World Health Organization (WHO) released its Global TB Report 2026. According to the notice, the report says TB deaths in India have fallen 38 per cent since 2015, while the global decline was 35 per cent.

The report puts TB deaths per lakh population at 28 in 2015, falling to 18 in 2025. WHO's South-East Asia Regional Office (SEARO) also acknowledged India's contribution in a social media post, the notice says.

## Testing and treatment

The notice says the treatment success rate among drug-sensitive TB patients is 94 per cent. It links this to better diagnosis, timely treatment and regular monitoring of patients.

Under the TB Mukt Bharat campaign, more than 25 crore people were screened, including over 11 crore chest X-rays and 3 crore molecular tests. This found 48.64 lakh TB cases in all, of which about 15 lakh people showed no common symptoms.

## What is helping

The notice mentions AI-based portable digital chest X-rays, rapid molecular testing technology and the Ni-kshay digital platform. It also points to nutrition support under the Ni-kshay Poshan Yojana and community help from Ni-kshay Mitras.

The campaign began in December 2024 as a 100-day intensified drive and was later expanded across the country. Another intensified campaign started in March 2026, with focus on early detection and timely treatment.

## What it means for ordinary people

This notice does not announce a new scheme to apply for, so readers have no form or application to submit. What it does say is that TB testing and treatment are already running in the government system, and people without visible symptoms are also being traced.

The notice also says 67,933 gram panchayats were declared TB-free in 2025, and that 96 per cent of the programme's total funding need comes from domestic resources. The minister repeated that the aim is that no TB patient is left out of treatment and care.
