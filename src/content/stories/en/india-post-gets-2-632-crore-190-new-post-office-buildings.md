## What has happened

The Union Cabinet has approved continuing two India Post plans. Together they will cost ₹2,632 crore over the 16th Finance Commission cycle, that is, from financial year 2026-27 to 2030-31.

Of this, ₹1,141 crore is for the Postal Operations Plan and ₹1,491 crore for the Estate Management Plan. The details were shared at a press conference by Union Communications Minister Jyotiraditya M. Scindia, Minister of State for Communications and Rural Development Dr Chandra Sekhar Pemmasani, and Postal Secretary Subrata Das.

## Where the money will go

The Postal Operations Plan focuses on six areas. These include reaching rural postal services, improving the mail network and the parcel network, international postal infrastructure, marketing and service quality, and activities related to stamp collecting (philately).

The Estate Management Plan will focus on modernising post office buildings. Under it, 190 new departmental postal buildings will be built and 50 buildings reconstructed. As many as 2,550 departmental sub-post offices will be renovated and branded, and 2,500 rented post offices will be upgraded. Twenty heritage postal buildings will be conserved.

The plan will also support the renovation of 150 residential colonies and the development of a permanent integrated technology centre for CEPT in Bengaluru.

## What changes for customers

The press conference was told that the Integrated Insurance Management System (IMS) lets a customer complete the whole process of buying a PLI policy in about 20 minutes. So far about 2 lakh customers' premium transactions have been handled through this system, bringing in about ₹60 crore in revenue.

The Postal Secretary said the department aims to raise India Post's annual revenue to about ₹19,803 crore in financial year 2026-27. This will be done by improving operational efficiency, expanding parcel and logistics services, adopting technology and engaging better with customers.

## What ordinary people need to do

There is no form to fill or application to make for this. It is a plan to improve the postal department's own buildings and services. When a post office in your area is built or repaired, services such as accounts, insurance, parcels and mail should be available in better conditions.
