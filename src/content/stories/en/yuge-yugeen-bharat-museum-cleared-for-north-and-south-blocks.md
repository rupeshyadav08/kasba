## What was approved

The Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA), chaired by Prime Minister Narendra Modi, on 11 November 2026 approved the development of the 'Yuge Yugeen Bharat Museum' (YYBM) in the North and South Block complex in New Delhi. The estimated total cost of the project is ₹3,230 crore, appraised by the Public Investment Board (PIB).

## What kind of museum it will be

The museum is planned as a civilisational museum that will show India's cultural traditions, knowledge systems, artistic traditions and heritage. It will use modern museum practices, immersive technologies and visitor-focused presentation. According to the release, it will have the largest display area of any museum in the world.

## What happens to the old buildings

The North and South Blocks are historic buildings. After government offices moved out, an opportunity came to reuse them for a major public cultural purpose. The buildings will be developed as a museum while preserving their architectural features and historic value. The proposed built-up area is about 1,78,509 square metres.

## How long it will take

The project will be implemented in phases from financial year 2026-27 to 2030-31. The first gallery, based on the theme of ornamentation in North Block, is expected to open to the public in the latter part of 2027. Other galleries will cover themes such as architecture and urban landscapes, mathematics and astronomy, languages and scripts, faith and spirituality, and India's connections through trade and ideas.

## What will be on display

About 18 lakh artefacts have been identified from the National Museum and other museums under the Culture Ministry, 52 site museums of the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI), and various state museums and private collections. This material is being analysed.

## What it means for ordinary people

There is nothing for the public to do right now, and no application or last date. Once the museum is ready, tourists, students and researchers visiting Delhi will be able to see it. According to the release, the museum is being designed to handle one crore visitors a year.
