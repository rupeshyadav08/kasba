## What was launched

Union Health Secretary Punya Salila Srivastava launched an initiative called 'NCG Vishwas' on 21 November 2026 at the annual meeting 2026 of the National Cancer Grid (NCG) in Mumbai. It is an AI-based patient education initiative with videos on cancer. The videos cover breast, cervical and oral cancer and explain prevention, diagnosis, treatment and long-term care.

## In which languages and where

The videos are in Hindi, Marathi, Tamil and English. The Health Secretary said they will be spread through the NCG network of hospitals and healthcare partners. They can also reach people through ASHA and Anganwadi workers and Ayushman Arogya camps, and can be made available on platforms such as ABDM and the Swasth Bharat portal.

## Focus on screening and records

The Health Secretary said screening for common non-communicable diseases, including oral, breast and cervical cancer, is being done for people above 30 at 1.87 lakh Ayushman Arogya Mandirs across the country. More than 153 crore screenings have been done so far, including 37.1 crore for oral cancer, 17.4 crore for breast cancer and 9.7 crore for cervical cancer.

She said the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM) links screening data to ABHA IDs. About 99 crore ABHA IDs have been created and about 100 crore health records linked. With the patient's consent, records should be available to providers at different levels so care continues without a break.

## Cost of treatment and facilities

She referred to steps such as rationalising trade margins on cancer drugs. She said vulnerable and marginalised patients get financial support under AB PM-JAY, and about Rs 27,000 crore has been provided for cancer treatment under the scheme. Screening and follow-up are recorded on the national programme portal, confirmation is mainly done at district hospitals, and treatment is provided through medical colleges and AB PM-JAY.

## What the NCG is

The National Cancer Grid is a network of more than 400 cancer centres, research institutions and patient groups. It works to improve cancer care through standardised protocols, quality assurance, capacity building and collaborative research.
