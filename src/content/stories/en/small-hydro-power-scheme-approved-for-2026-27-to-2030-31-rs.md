## What has been approved

The central government has approved the Small Hydro Power (SHP) development scheme for financial years 2026-27 to 2030-31. It carries a budgetary provision of Rs 2,584.60 crore and aims to add about 1,500 MW of small hydro capacity.

Small hydro uses the natural flow of rivers and canals to generate electricity. Projects with installed capacity up to 25 MW fall in this category and are administered by the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE).

## Who benefits

The scheme gives priority to hill, north-eastern and border areas. In north-eastern states and districts along the international border, assistance is Rs 3.6 crore per MW or 30 per cent of project cost, whichever is less, capped at Rs 30 crore per project.

At other locations, assistance is Rs 2.4/1.78/2.054 crore per MW (depending on project size and location) or 20 per cent of project cost, whichever is less, with a cap of Rs 20 crore per project.

## Project preparation and jobs

Under the scheme, help will be given to prepare detailed project reports (DPRs) for at least 200 projects. A separate allocation of Rs 30 crore is meant to help central and state agencies build a pipeline of future projects.

According to the notice, the scheme is expected to attract about Rs 15,000 crore of investment in the small hydro sector. It is expected to create about 51 lakh person-days of employment during construction, with continuing jobs in operation and maintenance.

## How much capacity India has

India has identified a potential small hydro capacity of 21,134 MW at 7,133 sites. Up to 30 September 2026, about 5,182 MW, or roughly 25 per cent, had been harnessed. The remaining 15,952 MW is still available to be developed.

Region-wise, the northern region has 8,030 MW, the north-east 2,995 MW, the southern region 5,490 MW, the western region 2,912 MW and the eastern region 1,707 MW.

## What ordinary people need to do

The notice gives no application, registration or deadline for ordinary citizens. The scheme works by supporting projects through central and state agencies, so readers do not need to take any action.
