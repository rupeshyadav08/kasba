## What happened

Union Minister for Environment, Forest and Climate Change Bhupendra Yadav visited the new Integrated Command and Control Centre of the Commission for Air Quality Management (CAQM) in New Delhi on 30 October 2026. The centre has been named 'Pawan'. Its full name is Platform for Air Quality Vigilance and Analytics for NCR. The minister reviewed its technical structure and operational preparations.

## What Pawan-I3C does

It is a single-window digital control centre. It integrates more than 30 API and data sources from different agencies, including data on air quality, emissions, weather, satellites, cameras, traffic, complaints and compliance. It also brings together data from 111 continuous ambient air quality monitoring stations in Delhi-NCR, more than 2,300 industries, more than 3,200 active construction sites and key traffic congestion points.

## What changes

The minister said CAQM's working so far relied on manual monitoring and reports given by agencies. With Pawan-I3C, it moves to active, digital and data-based compliance management. Alerts will be issued when there is a deviation from set standards or directions, and sent to the concerned agency for action. This will help state pollution control boards work from one shared and verified source of information, and polluters can be held accountable within minutes.

## Who is affected

The minister said construction and demolition (C&D) activities will be stopped across the entire NCR region from 5 to 20 November to control pollution. This directly affects people and firms doing construction or demolition work. He also said early action will be taken against industries not meeting standards, polluting sources that break rules and traffic congestion points.

## What was reviewed

The visit covered data on air quality and pollutant concentrations, industrial emission data, satellite-based stubble burning data including comparison with fire incidents of the last six years, live camera feeds and PM sensors at construction sites, and weather, rainfall, wind speed and direction and humidity data. It also covered live feeds from major landfill sites including Okhla, Bhalswa, Ghazipur, Greater Noida and Bhandwari, vehicle data from the Vahan portal, citizen complaints through the Green Delhi app and Sameer app, live monitoring of mechanical road sweeping machines, and compliance by brick kilns in Haryana and Punjab with biomass fuel requirements.

## What it means for ordinary people

Ordinary residents do not have to fill any form or submit anything by a deadline. The change mainly concerns the agencies that monitor pollution and take action. If there is pollution around you, a complaint can be filed on the Green Delhi app or the Sameer app.
