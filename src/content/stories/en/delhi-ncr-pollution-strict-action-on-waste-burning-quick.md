## What happened at the meeting

Union Minister for Environment, Forest and Climate Change Bhupendra Yadav held a virtual review meeting with civic bodies of Delhi, Ghaziabad, Noida, Gurugram and Faridabad on 19 November 2026, joining from Antalya in Turkiye. The meeting reviewed preparations and implementation of winter action plans to curb air pollution in Delhi-NCR. The minister said clear and continuous action on the ground is needed this winter.

The meeting was attended by Minister of State Kirti Vardhan Singh, ministry secretary Narendra Bhushan, CAQM chairperson Rajesh Verma, CPCB chairperson Dr Amandeep Garg, mayors and commissioners of the municipal corporations concerned, the Noida Authority CEO and senior state officials.

## Where enforcement will be tighter

The minister directed civic bodies to adopt a zero-tolerance policy on open burning of municipal waste, dry leaves and organic matter, with immediate punitive action against violators. A quick response team is to be set up in every ward so complaints are acted on within hours. Officials were also told they would be held accountable for failure in enforcement.

On waste, the directions were to fully process daily waste and speed up clearing of legacy waste, with priority to the Bhalaswa and Okhla dump sites.

## Road dust and construction sites

On road dust, the minister asked for faster road repairs, a big increase in the capacity of mechanical road sweeping machines and their full use, including running them in two shifts during winter. He stressed GPS-based monitoring, supervision through the Integrated Command and Control Centre (ICCC) and daily removal of dust, silt and construction debris from roadsides.

At construction and demolition (C&D) sites, dust control norms must be strictly followed, with wheel-washing of vehicles and regular inspections. Sites that do not follow rules seriously may face strong action, including closure. The minister said no dust should be visible at or around construction sites.

## Hotspots and accountability

Reviewing pollution hotspots and GRAP implementation, the minister asked civic bodies to appoint dedicated nodal officers and prepare separate action plans for each hotspot. All measures under the current stage of GRAP must be enforced without exception. District magistrates were told to regularly review the performance of civic bodies and take corrective action against those lagging.

Complaints from the public should be monitored in real time and closed only after physical verification and resolution, the minister said. He also asked civic bodies to adopt successful NCR initiatives such as the Delhi municipal corporation's malba app, Faridabad's pothole ambulance, Gurugram's survey-based identification of sanitation workers and Ghaziabad's shared C&D waste portal.
