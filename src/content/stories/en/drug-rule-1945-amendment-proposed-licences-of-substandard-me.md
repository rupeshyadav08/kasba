## What has changed

The Ministry of Health and Family Welfare has issued a draft amendment to the Drugs Rules, 1945. The aim is to allow immediate suspension of the manufacturing licence of a medicine that fails the prescribed quality standards.

According to the ministry, current rules require a set process before a licence is suspended or cancelled, including issuing a 'show cause notice'. This can leave a gap between a drug being declared not of standard quality (NSQ) and action being taken against it. During that time the medicine may stay in the market, which can put public health at risk and make a recall harder.

## Who benefits

Under the proposal, the Central Licensing Approval Authority or the State Licensing Authority, as applicable, can suspend the manufacturing licence at once on receiving a government analyst's report. The report must declare the drug NSQ on testing, dissolution, related substances, impurities or sterility.

The ministry says the changes are expected to strengthen monitoring of medicines in the market, allow timely action against low-quality drugs and raise accountability of manufacturers and sellers.

## Conditions for restoring a licence

The suspension will continue until the manufacturer carries out a root cause analysis of the quality failure and applies corrective and preventive action (CAPA) to the satisfaction of the licensing authority. The authority will verify these steps before the licence is restored.

## Where the report goes

Changes are also proposed in 'Form 13', which relates to the government analyst's test report. Under the proposed system, the report will be sent to the Drugs Controller General of India (DCGI), the licensing authority of the concerned state or union territory, and the manufacturer and marketer of the drug.

## How to send suggestions

The draft has been issued for public consultation. Objections and suggestions can be sent within 30 days of the draft rules being made available to the public in the Gazette. These can be sent to the Under Secretary (Drugs), Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, U-6, Work Hall-C Wing, First Floor, Kartavya Bhawan-1, New Delhi-110001, or by e-mail to drugsdiv-mohfw[at]gov[dot]in. The draft Gazette notification can be seen on the official e-Gazette portal.

The ministry says the proposals are based on discussions at the 65th meeting of the Drugs Consultative Committee (DCC) held on 20 December 2024 and recommendations of the 92nd meeting of the Drugs Technical Advisory Board (DTAB) held on 24 April 2025.
