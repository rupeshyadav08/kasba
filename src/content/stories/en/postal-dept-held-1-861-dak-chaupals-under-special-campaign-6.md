## What happened

The Department of Posts, under the Ministry of Communications, held a one-day programme across the country on 23 October 2026 as part of Special Campaign 6. During this, 1,861 Dak Chaupals were organised. According to the release, the aim was to strengthen citizen participation and bring postal and government services closer to people.

## Who asked for it

The programme was held on the initiative of the Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances (DARPG). Its focus was reaching citizens, resolving grievances, cleanliness, environmental sustainability and improving postal infrastructure.

## What happened at the Chaupals

A Dak Chaupal is a platform for direct dialogue between postal officials and citizens. Awareness was raised about postal and citizen-centric services, people's complaints were heard and feedback was taken to improve service delivery. The Chaupals also spread awareness about Postal Life Insurance (PLI) and Rural Postal Life Insurance (RPLI) schemes.

## What happened to complaints

During the programme, 9,007 public complaints were resolved. The release describes this as reflecting the department's commitment to addressing citizens' concerns on time.

## Cleanliness and infrastructure

Special cleanliness drives were held at all post offices, including branch post offices, mail offices and administrative establishments. Cleanliness work was done at a total of 55,952 locations. Postal circles also ran initiatives on waste segregation, composting and responsible waste management.

Postal establishments took steps to improve office infrastructure, make public areas more attractive and use available space better. Creative beautification, maintenance and other improvement work made post offices cleaner and more attractive.

## What next

The release says the Department of Posts will continue these initiatives so that postal establishments become cleaner, more accessible and more responsive to people's needs. It does not give a date for the next Chaupal.
