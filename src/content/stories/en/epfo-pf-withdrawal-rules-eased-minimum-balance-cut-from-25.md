## What happened at the meeting

Union Labour and Employment Minister Dr Mansukh Mandaviya chaired the 240th meeting of the Central Board of Trustees (CBT) of the Employees' Provident Fund in New Delhi on 19 November 2026. Co-chair Dr Chandra Bhushan Kumar and Member Secretary Shri Ramesh Krishnamurthy attended, along with representatives of central and state governments, employers and employees. The board took up proposals on pension reform, flexibility in the provident fund, investment management, governance, exemption policy and EPFO's digital transformation.

## What changed in withdrawal rules

The board approved two changes linked to final PF withdrawal. Earlier, a member had to keep 25% of the amount deposited in the account as a minimum balance at all times. This requirement has now been cut to 10%. The waiting period for final withdrawal has also been reduced from 12 months to 2 months. The government says this gives members more financial flexibility while protecting their long-term social security interests.

## What is the SWP

The board also approved a Systematic Withdrawal Plan. Under it, retiring EPF members can take their savings in instalments at intervals of their choice — monthly, quarterly or half-yearly — instead of withdrawing everything at once. The remaining amount keeps earning returns and the member retains ownership of the corpus. The option is fully voluntary and can also be offered to eligible family members or beneficiaries.

## Who benefits

In the initial phase, about 6.32 lakh EPF members above 55 years with more than ₹5 lakh in their provident fund will be the main beneficiaries of the proposed SWP framework. Other PF members retiring later may also choose it. The proposal includes UAN-linked arrangements that let members make voluntary contributions and third-party micro-contributions (such as tips from customers) independent of an employer payroll system.

## What happens next

The necessary changes to the EPF Scheme will be notified after approval from the ministry. A similar change to the Income Tax Act is being discussed with the Finance Ministry. The new rules will apply only once the official notification comes.

## Other decisions

The board approved amendments to the EDLI Scheme 2026 to settle old pending exemption cases. Eligible establishments will get six months to renew lapsed exemptions and relief from contributions, interest and damages for a verifiable transition period. Separately, proposals for 27 establishments to surrender exemptions and four to have them cancelled will move about ₹5,996 crore in PF savings to EPFO, covering 1,44,437 members. After CITES 2.0, the share of auto-processed claims rose from 39% to 71%, the claim rejection rate fell from 23% to 7.82%, and average settlement time dropped from 11.15 days to 2.57 days. The board also approved revised estimates for 2026-27 and budget estimates for 2027-28.
