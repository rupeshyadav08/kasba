## What has happened

The Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT) under the Commerce Department has banned the export of lithium-ion battery black mass. Through Notification No. 49/2026-27 dated 10 November 2026, the item under ITC (HS) code 85491400 has been moved from the 'free' category to the 'restricted' category in the export policy. The change applies with immediate effect and until further orders.

## What black mass is

Black mass is a powder-like material produced by cutting and grinding used lithium-ion batteries and waste from battery manufacturing. It contains recoverable amounts of critical minerals such as lithium, cobalt, nickel and manganese. These minerals are key inputs for electric mobility, battery manufacturing, energy storage, electronics and renewable energy.

## Why the step was taken

According to the government, India depends heavily on imports for the supply of many of these minerals. The country's capacity to recycle battery waste and refine critical minerals from it has grown, but it is not being fully used because enough raw material is not available. Exporting black mass produced in India sends this valuable secondary resource abroad, and in some cases domestic recycling units have had to import material to meet their needs.

## Who is expected to benefit

The government expects the ban to ensure feedstock for domestic recycling and refining units, better use of their capacity, and more investment and jobs in the sector. It is also expected to increase domestic recovery of lithium, cobalt, nickel and other critical minerals, reducing import dependence and related foreign exchange spending.

## Link with other government schemes

The step is described as complementing initiatives under the National Critical Mineral Mission (NCMM). These include the Rs 1,500 crore incentive scheme approved by the Union Cabinet in September 2025, aimed at building recycling capacity to separate and produce critical minerals from secondary sources such as battery waste and e-waste. Keeping black mass within the country is expected to help ensure enough domestic feedstock for that capacity.

## What it means for ordinary people

This is an export policy decision whose direct effect is on traders and recycling units dealing in black mass. Ordinary consumers have no application to file and no deadline to meet. The full notification is available on the DGFT website, www.dgft.gov.in.
