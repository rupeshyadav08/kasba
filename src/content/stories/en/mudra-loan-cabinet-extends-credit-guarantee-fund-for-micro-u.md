## What was decided

The Union Cabinet, chaired by the Prime Minister, has approved continuing the Credit Guarantee Fund for Micro Units (CGFMU) for five years, till 31 March 2031. A corpus of Rs 5,000 crore has also been approved.

The fund was launched in January 2016 and has since helped in lending under the Pradhan Mantri Mudra Yojana (PMMY).

## How the guarantee works

Under the scheme, credit guarantee cover is given to lending institutions through the National Credit Guarantee Trustee Company Ltd (NCGTC). In simple terms, if a loan is not repaid, part of the risk is backed by the guarantee, so lenders feel safer giving small loans without collateral.

The guarantee coverage is linked to the lender's actual risk — the amount disbursed in working capital limits and the outstanding amount in term loan accounts.

## Which loans are covered

- Loans up to Rs 20 lakh given to eligible micro enterprises under PMMY.
- Overdraft facility up to Rs 10,000 given by banks under PM Jan Dhan Yojana accounts.
- Loans to self-help groups (SHGs) whose portfolio is between Rs 10 lakh and Rs 20 lakh.

## What has changed

The guarantee fee has been reduced from 1 per cent a year to 0.75 per cent a year. There is no change for self-help groups — they will continue to pay 0.25 per cent in the first year and 0.50 per cent in later years.

The scheme will apply to all PMMY loans given during the 16th Finance Commission period, that is, from financial year 2026-27 to 2030-31.

## Who benefits

According to the release, collateral-free loans under the Mudra Yojana have helped build a new generation of entrepreneurs and brought people into formal banking who earlier had no access to it.

The extension is expected to help provide Rs 12.6 lakh crore of loans over the next five years. The government says this will encourage young and ambitious entrepreneurs to start and grow their businesses.

## What you should do

The release does not give any separate application process or date for the public. If you want a loan under the Mudra Yojana, talk to your bank or lending institution.
