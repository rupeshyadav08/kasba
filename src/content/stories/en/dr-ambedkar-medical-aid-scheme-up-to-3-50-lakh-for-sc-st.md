The Dr Ambedkar Foundation (DAF), an autonomous body under the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment, is implementing the revised Dr Ambedkar Medical Aid Scheme. According to the Foundation's Director, Shri Vinesh Pachnanda, the revised scheme was notified on 2 June 2026. The aim is to make sure that a shortage of money does not come in the way of necessary treatment or surgery.

## Who can get the aid

The scheme covers Scheduled Caste (SC) and Scheduled Tribe (ST) patients whose annual family income is not more than ₹5 lakh. It includes serious illnesses of the kidney, heart, liver, brain and cancer. Organ transplant, spine surgery and other life-threatening illnesses that need medical or surgical treatment are also covered.

## How much is given

The scheme sets a maximum amount for each type of illness. Heart surgery gets up to ₹1.50 lakh, kidney surgery or dialysis up to ₹3.50 lakh, cancer surgery, chemotherapy or radiotherapy up to ₹2.50 lakh, brain surgery up to ₹2 lakh, kidney or organ transplant up to ₹3.50 lakh, spine surgery up to ₹1.50 lakh and other life-threatening illnesses up to ₹2 lakh.

The money does not go to the patient. Within the fixed limit, 100 per cent of the estimated cost of the treatment is paid directly to the hospital concerned. It can be released before surgery as a one-time instalment through RTGS, NEFT or demand draft.

## What is new in the revised scheme

Keeping in mind long-drawn treatment, patients with chronic illnesses can get a second renewal grant in the next financial year, subject to the applicable provisions. Also, PM-JAY beneficiaries who have kidney disease or need a kidney or other organ transplant can get aid under this scheme. Other PM-JAY patients are not eligible for aid under this scheme.

## Which hospitals are recognised

The scheme runs through a wide network of recognised health institutions. These include AIIMS New Delhi and AIIMS in other states, all hospitals recognised under the Central Government Health Scheme (CGHS), hospitals attached to state government medical colleges, state government hospitals, hospitals recognised by state governments and hospitals fully funded by the central or state governments.

Government hospitals in district headquarters and major cities that offer treatment for serious illnesses, including organ transplant and spine surgery, are also included. The list includes institutions such as Sanjay Gandhi Postgraduate Institute of Medical Sciences, Lucknow; Patna Medical College Hospital, Patna; and Jabalpur Hospital and Research Centre, Jabalpur. In special circumstances, treatment at other hospitals may also be considered, but this needs the satisfaction of the Foundation's Chairman.

## How to apply

Eligible beneficiaries can apply using the prescribed form available on the Foundation's website, https://www.dosje.gov.in/schemes-and-services/dr-ambedkar-medical-aid-scheme/. An application can also be submitted on plain paper with the required details in the prescribed format.

The application must carry self-attested copies of the patient's latest income certificate, caste certificate, ration card or Aadhaar card and a passport-size photograph. The original estimated cost certificate signed by the hospital's medical superintendent is also required, stating the estimated cost of treatment and the scheduled date of surgery. For kidney transplant cases, applicants must also give documents about the relationship between donor and recipient, Form-14 on the Authorisation Committee's decision and relevant details of the kidney donor.

The complete application must reach the Director, Dr Ambedkar Foundation, 15 Janpath, New Delhi-110001 at least 15 days before the scheduled date of surgery, dialysis or chemotherapy. Applications for reimbursement of money already spent are generally not considered, though these may be considered in special circumstances.

## Conditions for hospitals

Hospitals must submit a utilisation certificate along with the final medical bills when the patient is discharged. The aid released must be used within one month of the date of release. Any amount not spent or left over must be returned to the Foundation as early as possible, and in any case within one month of the patient's discharge.
