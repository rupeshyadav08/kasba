## What the fair is and where it is

The Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment has organised a 15-day Shilp Samagam Mela 2026 at Delhi Haat in INA, Delhi. According to the ministry, the fair is a platform to showcase India's traditional crafts, cultural heritage and entrepreneurship. It will run till 15 November 2026.

## How much has been sold

A senior official of the ministry said the four corporations taking part recorded total sales of about Rs 64.52 lakh between 1 and 9 November 2026. The four are the National Scheduled Castes Finance and Development Corporation (NSFDC), the National Backward Classes Finance and Development Corporation (NBCFDC), the National Safai Karamcharis Finance and Development Corporation (NSKFDC) and the National Divyangjan Finance and Development Corporation (NDFDC). The official said sales have been encouraging from the start.

## Who benefits

According to the ministry, the fair gives artisans and beneficiaries linked to Scheduled Caste, Other Backward Class, sanitation worker and divyangjan communities a chance to show and sell their products to a wider audience. The ministry has been holding such Shilp Samagam fairs since 2022. Through them, more than 1,000 artisans from these communities have so far got a chance to display and sell their goods. The aim is to promote self-employment, encourage entrepreneurship and help these communities become stronger socially and economically.

## What is at the fair

The fair has 7 thematic zones: Kala Aangan, Prakash Chowk, Pooja Path, Sajawat Bazaar, Gharana, Swad Chowk and Manch. More than 130 stalls display handicrafts, handloom products, traditional textiles, festival decorations, lamps, home decor items and other indigenous products. The theme is 'Prakash aur Parampara - Jyoti se Jyoti Jalayein'. At the Manch, artistes from different states perform folk dances, traditional music and regional arts every evening.

## What support artisans get

The ministry makes participation easier by providing beneficiaries free stalls, travel expenses, a daily allowance and transport for their goods. According to the ministry, this helps artisans with the cost and trouble of travelling and carrying their products. The fair connects them directly with customers. Besides, initiatives such as Bharat Tulip link artisans to e-commerce platforms, and initiatives such as Purple Fest celebrate the creativity and abilities of divyangjan.

## What it means for ordinary people

If you live in or near Delhi and want to buy handicrafts or traditional items, you can visit Delhi Haat till 15 November 2026. The ministry has not said whether there is an entry ticket for the fair. That information can be checked with Delhi Haat or the ministry.
