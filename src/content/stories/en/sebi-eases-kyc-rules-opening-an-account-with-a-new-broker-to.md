## What has happened

Market regulator SEBI issued a circular on 25 November 2026 changing how KYC Registration Agencies (KRAs) work. KRAs are entities that hold investors' KYC records and share them with companies in the securities market.

SEBI said a working group of market participants, KRAs and industry associations was set up to review the existing risk management framework at KRAs and streamline client on-boarding. The changes follow its recommendations, suggestions from public consultation and discussions with KRAs.

## What has changed

Some supplementary information in Part II of the account opening form (AOF), which is common across intermediaries, will now be centralised at the KRA level. This will make on-boarding easier when a client approaches another intermediary.

Intermediaries must also upload the CKYC ID and DIGIPIN (if available) and the expiry or validity date of the Officially Valid Document to the KRA system. This information must be uploaded within three working days of completing the KYC process. It includes income range, FATCA and CRS related information, PEP status, occupation and the document's expiry date.

## Mobile number and record updates

If a client's mobile number is tagged by the KRA as seeded with Aadhaar and verified, verification of that number will be optional for the intermediary.

Intermediaries will update KYC records based on information received from the KRA and the Central KYC Records Registry, to avoid duplication of effort. Clients will be given an option to confirm the update in their KYC records.

## What happens when an account is closed

Intermediaries other than mutual funds must inform the KRA within five working days of an account closure. Within three months of the circular, they must also inform KRAs about closures of existing KYC records linked to them. The KRA must update or delink the record within two working days of receiving the information.

For mutual funds, if a client account across folios has zero balance for six months, the fund or its RTA must notify the KRA within five working days. The KRA will then stop sending KYC information updates to that fund or its RTA.

## What it means for an ordinary investor

The change is mainly about how companies and KRAs handle records. An ordinary investor does not have to fill a separate form or meet any new deadline. The benefit is that some details will not have to be given again when opening an account with another company.

If your KYC record is updated, the intermediary will give you an option to confirm it. For more details, contact your broker, mutual fund or depository participant.
