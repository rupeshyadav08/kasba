## What has happened

According to the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, more than 1 crore doses of the HPV vaccine have been given under the National HPV Vaccination Campaign in less than nine months. The ministry called it a major step in protecting adolescent girls from cervical cancer. Prime Minister Narendra Modi, Vice-President C. P. Radhakrishnan and Union Health Minister Jagat Prakash Nadda appreciated the achievement, and the World Health Organization's South-East Asia region congratulated India.

## What the campaign is

The campaign was launched by Prime Minister Narendra Modi in February 2026. Under it, 14-year-old girls are given a free, safe and voluntary single-dose HPV vaccine at government health centres. A parent's consent is required for the shot.

## How many girls have been reached

The ministry said the campaign has achieved about 87 per cent coverage of the yearly target group of nearly 1.2 crore girls aged 14. Six states — Gujarat, Uttar Pradesh, Andhra Pradesh, Mizoram, Madhya Pradesh and Bihar — have reached 100 per cent coverage of their target population, and 12 states have crossed 80 per cent coverage.

Uttar Pradesh leads in the number of girls vaccinated, with over 29.33 lakh, followed by Bihar (over 13.70 lakh) and Gujarat (over 7.75 lakh).

## Why the vaccine matters

Persistent infection with some high-risk types of HPV is responsible for most cases of cervical cancer. The ministry says HPV vaccination is among the most effective ways to prevent such infections. More than 800 million doses of the vaccine have been given worldwide, and about two decades of research and real-world experience show it gives strong protection against HPV infection and pre-cancerous conditions. The shot is given at age 14 so that protection comes well before exposure to the virus.

## What safety arrangements are in place

Vaccination sessions are held at government health centres under the direct supervision of trained medical officers. These centres are linked to health facilities that work 24x7, so any rare adverse event following immunisation can be assessed, monitored and managed immediately.

## What you should do

Parents and guardians can visit their nearest government health centre for information and to book a vaccination slot, or use the U-WIN platform. A digital vaccination certificate can also be downloaded from U-WIN.
