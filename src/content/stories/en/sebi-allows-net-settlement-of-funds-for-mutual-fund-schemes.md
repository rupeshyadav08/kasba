## What has happened

SEBI issued a circular on November 5, 2026 permitting net settlement of funds for outright transactions undertaken by mutual fund schemes in the cash market. The framework comes into force on April 1, 2027.

Under the current rules, institutional investors cannot do day trading, and their cash market transactions are delivery-backed and settled on a gross basis at the custodian or client level. The new decision changes only how funds are settled, not how securities are settled.

## What changes

A mutual fund scheme can now net purchase and sale values of outright transactions within the same settlement cycle. An outright transaction means either a purchase or a sale of a security, not both.

If a scheme has both purchase and sale transactions in the same security within the same settlement cycle, those are treated as non-outright and stay outside netting; they are funded separately on a gross basis. The framework does not apply to derivatives, securities lending and borrowing, short sale-related transactions or anything else not expressly covered.

## Who benefits

SEBI said the aim is to enhance operational efficiency, reduce temporary funding requirements and improve settlement efficiency for mutual fund schemes. According to the circular, a scheme's funding requirement is reduced to the extent netting is permitted.

## What safeguards apply

Netting is allowed only within one scheme. There is no netting across different schemes of the same mutual fund and no adjustment between two or more schemes or portfolios. AMCs and custodians must ensure this does not affect scheme-wise accounting, NAV computation, asset allocation, investment limits or unit-holder interest.

If outright sale value is less than outright purchase value, the scheme funds the residual obligation. If sale value exceeds purchase value, the excess is not adjusted against non-outright purchase obligations. Securities settlement with the custodian stays on a gross basis, and STT and stamp duty continue to be charged on delivery basis.

## What happens next

AMFI, in consultation with clearing corporations, stock exchanges, custodians and other stakeholders, will frame implementation standards. These will cover file formats, settlement timelines, reconciliation, audit trail and handling of rejected or unconfirmed trades.

AMCs and custodians must jointly confirm system readiness before implementation, and trustees will review how the framework is implemented. The circular is available on www.sebi.gov.in under Legal — Circulars.
