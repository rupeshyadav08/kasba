## What has happened

The RBI on November 27, 2026 issued amended KYC directions. Their aim is to set a fixed procedure for banks to act on amounts or accounts suspected of money mule activity and cyber-enabled financial fraud.

The RBI had issued draft directions on September 11, 2026 to seek feedback. The feedback was examined and the changes were included in the final directions.

## What changes

The new directions set out a Standard Operating Procedure (SOP). Under it, banks may suo moto place temporary debit holds on amounts or accounts suspected to be linked to money mule activity or cyber-enabled financial fraud.

Such holds are to be removed in coordination with Law Enforcement Agencies (LEAs). The directions also say genuine customers should be protected from inconvenience.

## When it starts

The directions come into effect from July 1, 2027. A bank may, however, decide to implement the SOP from an earlier date.

## Who it covers

The RBI issued separate amended directions for commercial banks, small finance banks, payments banks, local area banks, regional rural banks and urban co-operative banks.

## What it means for ordinary customers

If an account is suspected of money mule activity, the bank may temporarily stop money from being taken out or sent from it. The hold is removed once the suspicion is cleared.

The directions do not list any step for ordinary customers. The key point is not to let your bank account be used to move someone else's money.
