## What has happened

The Ministry of Women and Child Development and the Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship will jointly launch Navya 2.0 in Haridwar on 28 November 2026. The event is scheduled for 3:30 pm at the Uttarakhand Sanskrit Academy auditorium. Navya stands for promoting aspirations through vocational training for young women and adolescent girls.

## What Navya is

Navya is a joint initiative that brings together the systems of the women and child development ministry and the skill development ministry. It recognises that empowerment is not only about training: it also means encouraging aspirations, building confidence, developing abilities and helping young women make informed decisions about their future.

## What the pilot phase did

The pilot phase began on 24 June 2025 in Sonbhadra, Uttar Pradesh, and was planned to cover 27 aspirational districts in 19 states. About 1,200 girls were trained in job-oriented trades such as drone manufacturing and assembly technician, solar PV installation assistant and fashion designer. Digital and financial literacy, career preparation and entrepreneurship were also given special attention.

## What changes now

Based on the pilot's experience, Navya 2.0 will expand to 100 districts across 24 states and union territories, with a focus on aspirational and remote areas. The biggest change is in age: the eligible age group has been raised from 16-18 years to 16-25 years, so that more girls and young women can be covered.

Under the expanded programme, more vocational training will be offered in modern and non-traditional trades that industry needs. Access to jobs, apprenticeships and entrepreneurship opportunities will also be strengthened. A standard operating procedure (SOP) has been put in place to help districts coordinate and implement the programme.

## The role of technology

The Skill India Digital Hub (SIDH) will work as a digital platform, helping candidates through the whole journey from enrolment to training and certification. It will also allow real-time monitoring of registration, attendance, training progress and certification. Digitally verifiable certificates will help candidates prove their skills.

## Who benefits

The initiative is aimed especially at girls and young women in aspirational and underserved areas, so they can recognise their abilities and contribute to economic and social development. The notice does not say where or by when applications must be made; readers can ask the concerned ministry or the district skill development office for details.
