## What happened

The Postal Department released two commemorative postage stamps and a set of picture postcards on breast cancer awareness on 30 October 2026. The event was held on the occasion of International Breast Cancer Awareness Month. The stamps were released by Union Health Secretary Smt. Punya Salila Srivastava and Director General of Postal Services Smt. Manisha Sinha.

The initiative was conceived and started by the Postal Department. The Ministry of Health and Family Welfare provided technical input, reference material and background information for the health content on the stamps and postcards.

## Why it was considered necessary

According to the release, more than two lakh new breast cancer cases were reported in India in 2024, and it is estimated that one in every 29 women is at risk of the disease. The event was told that breast cancer is the most common cancer among women in India, accounting for more than 30% of all new cancer cases among women.

Officials said that despite growing awareness and available screening facilities, late detection of the disease remains a concern. That is why early detection and self-examination are being stressed.

## Where screening stands

Under the National Programme for Prevention and Control of Non-Communicable Diseases (NP-NCD), a total of 86.97 crore screenings for mouth, breast and cervical cancer have been done across the country. These include 49.02 crore screenings for mouth cancer, 22.90 crore for breast cancer and 14.05 crore for cervical cancer.

Of these, 5.50 lakh people are currently under treatment. This includes 2.70 lakh mouth cancer, 1.43 lakh breast cancer and 1.37 lakh cervical cancer patients.

## Where screening is available

According to the release, screening for mouth, breast and cervical cancer has been added along with primary health services at more than 1.8 lakh Ayushman Arogya Mandirs. The cancer care chain includes screening and referral at the primary health level, confirmation and treatment at district hospitals, state cancer institutes and tertiary cancer care centres.

Daycare cancer centres have also been set up to provide chemotherapy closer to patients' homes. Under the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission, the NP-NCD portal and ABHA ID help with access to health records and continuity of treatment.

## What ordinary people should do

The Health Secretary asked women to focus on prevention through regular screening and self-examination. She advised people to make greater use of government health services and digital health platforms for early detection and treatment.

The release also said it is important to remove fear and stigma around cancer and the barriers to reaching health services.
