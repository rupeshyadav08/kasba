## What has started

The Ministry of Tribal Affairs formally launched a project on 12 November 2026 at Odisha Central University in Koraput, Odisha. It will run with the support of CSIR-National Botanical Research Institute (CSIR-NBRI), Lucknow. The aim is to conserve medicinal plants in tribal areas and create livelihood options for local communities. The project is for three years.

## Where it will run

It covers 16 selected tribal hotspots across 13 states and union territories. These include Sonbhadra in Uttar Pradesh and Anuppur in Madhya Pradesh. Others are Alluri Sitarama Raju (Andhra Pradesh), Kokrajhar (Assam), Narayanpur (Chhattisgarh), Navsari (Gujarat), East Singhbhum (Jharkhand), Kodagu (Karnataka), Wayanad (Kerala), Kargil (Ladakh), Gadchiroli and Nashik (Maharashtra), Kandhamal, Balangir and Koraput (Odisha), and Dharchula (Uttarakhand).

## What the project will do

About 200–300 medicinal plant species will be documented. Around 30–40 rare, endangered and threatened species will be scientifically propagated. Sixteen community nurseries and 16 seed banks will be set up. Community-level training will be given for cultivating medicinal plants and for bio-resource based enterprises. The project will also distribute 40,000–50,000 high-quality planting materials.

## What happened in Sonbhadra

As part of early implementation, CSIR-NBRI did field work in Sonbhadra district in October 2026. The team spoke to 146 tribal people from 12 villages who hold traditional knowledge, and documented their traditional medicinal practices. About 200 medicinal and economically important plant samples were collected under 97 field numbers. Twenty-five live orchid samples were also collected for germplasm conservation at the CSIR-NBRI botanical garden.

## Who benefits

The project aims to build strong links between scientific institutions, Krishi Vigyan Kendras, Van Dhan Vikas Kendras, state tribal welfare and forest departments, and local communities. Special focus is on involving tribal farmers, traditional healers, women and rural youth in conservation, training, cultivation and value-chain development. The ministry wants to create a model that can be replicated elsewhere.

## What next

The event included a technical session titled "From Tribal Knowledge to Enterprise", where experts and stakeholders discussed ways to turn traditional knowledge and medicinal plant resources into lasting economic opportunities. A field visit to the Koraput tribal hotspot was planned for 13 November 2026. The notice does not give any application date or process for the public.
