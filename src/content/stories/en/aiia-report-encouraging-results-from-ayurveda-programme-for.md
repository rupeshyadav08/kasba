## What happened

The All India Institute of Ayurveda (AIIA), New Delhi, ran a community Ayurveda programme for elderly people in Haryana's Faridabad district. According to the institute, the results were encouraging. The project report has been handed to an expert committee.

AIIA is an autonomous institute under the Ministry of Ayush, working on education, research, clinical services and public health initiatives in Ayurveda.

## What was studied

The project was titled "Community Ayurveda intervention for physical and mental health promotion among the elderly population of Faridabad district, Haryana". Its aim was to study the possibilities of Ayurveda-based treatment at the community level and to assess ways to promote healthy ageing.

Under the institute's Public Health Initiative, 5,594 people aged 60 to 80 in Faridabad were screened over three months and given Ayurvedic medicines. In a separate dementia-focused part, 200 patients were screened over four months and given Ayurveda-based medicines.

## What the first checks found

The early assessment threw up several health challenges among the elderly. Vision problems were recorded in 67.73% of participants, physical inactivity in 43.05% and reduced cognitive ability in 6.85%. Many also had high blood pressure, raised random blood sugar, low bone mineral density, constipation and sleep problems.

## What improved

In the first part of the project, quality of life improved after a 60-day intervention. The total World Health Organization quality-of-life (WHOQOL) score rose from 292.0 to 326.3. All six Agni Bala parameters, which in Ayurveda relate to digestive and metabolic capacity, also improved. Bone mineral density showed improvement as well.

In the second part, focused on cognitive health, the Mini Mental State Examination (MMSE) score moved towards the normal level. The 6-CIT assessment also showed signs of better cognitive performance. The quality-of-life score in this part rose from 277.4 to 364.2. Levels of serotonin, acetylcholine, DHEA and vitamin D also improved.

## Why caution is needed

The release itself says these results give an important basis for understanding the possible usefulness of community-level Ayurveda interventions. But it adds that detailed scientific evaluation, continuous safety monitoring and further studies are needed to confirm the findings.

## What was said at the workshop

The results were presented at a workshop at AIIA on 24 October. Dr Kaustubh Upadhyay, adviser in the Ministry of Ayush, was chief guest and AIIA director Prof. (Vaidya) Pradeep Kumar Prajapati chaired the event.

Dr Upadhyay said meaningful social engagement with the elderly is needed to understand their needs better, and stressed encouraging them to take part in physical and mental activities. Prof. Prajapati appreciated the public health initiative team and stressed taking such efforts to more elderly people. A book, "Holistic Approach for Healthy Ageing", was also released at the event.
