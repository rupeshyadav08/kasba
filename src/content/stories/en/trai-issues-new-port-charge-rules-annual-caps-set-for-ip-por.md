## What has happened

The Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI) issued the Telecom Interconnection (Port Charges) (Third Amendment) Regulations 2026 on 9 November 2026. These lay down a framework for charges on IP-based interconnection ports. The framework will work alongside the existing provisions that apply to E1/Time Division Multiplexing (TDM) based interconnection ports. The amended regulations take effect from 1 January 2027.

## What interconnection means

In simple terms, interconnection is the arrangement by which two or more telecom networks join together. It lets calls and data flow from one company's network to another, so users of different service providers can reach each other. Under Section 11(1)(b) of the TRAI Act, 1997, TRAI has the power to set the terms of interconnection between service providers.

## What the old rules said

The Telecom Interconnection (Port Charges) Regulations, 2001 set out the charge a service provider seeking interconnection pays to the provider giving it, for setting up an interconnection link at its point. Those rules were built on older E1/TDM port technology, and the charges were last reviewed in 2012.

## What has changed

- The definition of a port has been amended so that IP ports are now included alongside the older E1 port (2.048 Mbps). IP port capacity will be stated in Mbps or Gbps.
- Maximum annual charges for IP ports are fixed: Rs 5.6 lakh for a 1 Gbps IP port and Rs 42.3 lakh for a 10 Gbps IP port.
- Providers may agree by mutual consent on a charge lower than the maximum. They can also make arrangements for IP ports of capacities other than 1 Gbps and 10 Gbps, with charges set transparently on the same method used for 1 Gbps and 10 Gbps ports.
- IP port charges apply equally to fixed-line, mobile, national long distance and international long distance services. Only E1 ports keep their previously fixed charges.
- To ensure the rules are followed, a graded financial disincentive applies for violation of specified provisions, up to Rs 15 lakh per service area for each violation.

## How the rules were made

TRAI began the review by issuing a pre-consultation paper on 3 April 2025 on reviewing its existing interconnection regulations. A consultation paper followed on 10 November 2025, with the last dates for written comments and counter-comments set at 15 December 2025 and 29 December 2025 after an extension. Seventeen stakeholders sent comments and nine sent counter-comments, and an open house discussion was held on 28 January 2026. TRAI finalised the amendment on the basis of these inputs and its own analysis.

## What it means for ordinary people

This is not a rule aimed at consumers. It concerns the charges telecom companies pay one another to connect their networks. The notice mentions no application, payment or deadline for ordinary users. The full text and explanatory memorandum are available on TRAI's website, www.trai.gov.in.
