In a release dated 10 November 2026, the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment shared details of the safety support sanitation workers receive under the Namaste scheme. The release uses the example of one sanitation worker to show how a safety kit and training affected his work and income.

## What is the Namaste scheme

Namaste stands for 'National Action for Mechanised Sanitation Ecosystem'. It is run by the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment. The scheme aims to give sanitation workers occupational safety, health safety and livelihood security.

## What changed

According to the release, in 2025 a sanitation worker received a full Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) kit under the scheme. The kit included a protective overall, strong gloves, gumboots, a face mask and a safety helmet. He was also given awareness training on safe sanitation practices and on protecting himself from work-related health risks.

## Who benefits

The scheme is for sanitation workers, especially those who do difficult work such as cleaning sewers and septic tanks. The release notes that constant contact with hazardous waste can cause skin infections and breathing problems. Safety equipment helps protect workers from these risks.

## What the example says

The release gives the example of a sanitation worker with the Puducherry municipal body, whose name has been changed. He worked for more than 15 years cleaning sewers and septic tanks. Earlier, without safety equipment, he fell ill repeatedly and treatment costs added to the family's burden.

According to the release, after receiving the kit and training his health improved, his absences from work fell and his medical expenses came down. His monthly income rose from about Rs 12,000 to Rs 16,000–18,000.

## What to do next

The release does not give any application process, date or contact. This news asks nothing of ordinary citizens; it is information about the support being provided under the scheme.
