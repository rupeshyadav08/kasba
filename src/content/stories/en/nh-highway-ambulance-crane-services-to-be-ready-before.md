## What has happened

The National Highways Authority of India (NHAI) has issued guidelines to strengthen emergency services on national highways. Under these, incident management services (IMS) will be ensured before any highway stretch is opened to traffic.

The aim is to improve road safety and give highway users timely help during accidents, vehicle breakdowns and other emergencies.

## What incident management services include

These services include the availability of ambulances, route patrol vehicles and tow-away cranes on national highways. According to the release, these services are made effective by linking on-ground teams to NHAI's 1033 national highway helpline and by coordinating with local police, fire services and hospitals.

## What applies where

On running national highway stretches, ambulances are placed at user-fee plazas or other suitable locations based on accident data and the need of that stretch.

For highways handed over to NHAI by the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways, ambulances will be deployed through a centralised agency, in line with powers given to NHAI's regional offices. Patrol vehicles and tow-away cranes will be arranged through separate tenders or as part of highway maintenance and strengthening works.

## Where NHAI collects toll

Some highway stretches are not handed over to NHAI but NHAI collects toll on them. On such stretches, IMS deployment will follow the procedure set by the Ministry. Ambulances will be deployed through NHAI's centralised agency in coordination with the Ministry's concerned regional office or NHIDCL, while patrol vehicles and tow-away cranes on these stretches will be arranged by the concerned authority.

## What it means for a traveller

This step is directly linked to people who travel on national highways. In an emergency, help such as an ambulance, patrol vehicle or crane is meant to be ready. Travellers do not need to apply for anything; if needed, they can contact NHAI's 1033 helpline.
