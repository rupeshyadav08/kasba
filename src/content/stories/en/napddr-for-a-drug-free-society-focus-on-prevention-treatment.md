## What has happened

The Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment said on 18 November 2026 that the National Action Plan for Drug Demand Reduction (NAPDDR) is being implemented to bring down the demand for drugs. According to the ministry, the aim is to deal with drug-related challenges in a broad and coordinated way.

## What the plan builds on

The ministry has been running a scheme to prevent alcohol and substance abuse since 1985-86. The release also cites Article 47 of the Constitution, which directs the state to try to prohibit the consumption of intoxicating drinks and drugs that are injurious to health, except for medicinal purposes.

## How big is the problem

According to the release, the first national survey on substance use in India was done in 2018 by the National Drug Dependence Treatment Centre (NDDTC) at AIIMS New Delhi, and its report came out in February 2019. The survey found that about 16 crore people consumed alcohol, and more than 5.70 crore of them needed help because of harmful use or dependence. About 3.10 crore people used cannabis products and 2.26 crore used opioids. The survey also found that more than seven crore people were affected by substance-use problems, including about 1.20 crore children and 58 lakh women.

## What the plan will do

NAPDDR is being implemented as a centrally sponsored scheme and also includes central sector components. It has two main objectives. The first is to reduce drug demand through prevention education, awareness, identification, counselling, treatment and rehabilitation. The second is to reduce the stigma and discrimination linked to addiction, which often stand in the way of social acceptance and of people rejoining society.

## Who benefits

The release says the plan recognises that treatment and rehabilitation alone are not enough. A social environment is also needed where people can seek help without fear of discrimination or exclusion. Awareness and prevention education help people, families and communities understand the harms of substance use. Timely identification and counselling, along with access to proper treatment and rehabilitation facilities, are also described as key parts of the national effort.

## What ordinary people need to do

The notice gives no application, form, deadline or contact for the public. It is an information release about government efforts, so readers do not have to act on it.
