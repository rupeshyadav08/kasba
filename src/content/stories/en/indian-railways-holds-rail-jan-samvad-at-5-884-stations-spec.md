## What happened

Indian Railways held 'Rail Jan Samvad' at 5,884 railway stations across the country on 22 October 2026. According to the Ministry of Railways, the idea was to talk directly with passengers and seek their views and suggestions on cleanliness, passenger facilities and service delivery.

Railway Board Chairman and CEO Satish Kumar interacted with stakeholders in New Delhi, while the Secretary of DARPAN took part in the event at Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus, Mumbai.

## What Special Campaign 6 is

The Jan Samvad is part of Special Campaign 6, which is being run from 2 to 31 October 2026. The ministry links it to the Special Campaign for Disposal of Pending Matters (SCDPM-6). According to the release, work under the campaign covered cleanliness, scrap disposal, e-waste management, clearing pending matters and simplifying processes.

## Who benefits

The effort is aimed at train passengers who have views on station cleanliness and facilities. The railway says the interaction gave it a chance to identify local issues and take suitable follow-up action to improve passenger services.

## What next

With the campaign ending on 31 October 2026, the railway says its focus stays on cleanliness, efficient use of resources, timely disposal of pending matters and better passenger services. It says it will consolidate the achievements and keep up the good systems adopted during the campaign.

## What passengers should do

The notice does not give passengers any form, application or date to act on. In other words, there is nothing ordinary travellers need to do right now. Those who want to raise a cleanliness or facility issue can ask railway staff or officials at their local station.
