## Who made the appeal

WWF-India's EISCP programme, which is supported by the Union Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change, has asked people in Delhi-NCR to celebrate a cracker-free Green Diwali. The appeal is part of a nationwide Green Diwali campaign.

## What the air looks like now

According to CPCB, Delhi's air quality is currently in the 'very poor' category. Forecasts from the air quality early warning system say it may reach the 'severe' category during the festival days. The Commission for Air Quality Management (CAQM) is enforcing measures under the Graded Response Action Plan (GRAP) across the NCR and surrounding areas so that the air does not get worse. People have been urged to support these efforts and follow the measures.

## When and where the campaign ran

Under Mission LiFE, the campaign ran from 20 October to 8 November 2026. It focused especially on 130 cities covered by the National Clean Air Programme (NCAP) in 24 states and union territories.

## Simple steps for a Green Diwali

The appeal lists some easy steps. Choose traditional clay diyas and eco-friendly candles, and avoid heavy energy use. Give locally made, eco-friendly gifts with minimal packaging. Avoid firecrackers, as this cuts air and noise pollution and protects people's health.

For decoration, make rangoli with natural things like flower petals, rice flour and turmeric, and try DIY decoration by giving old items a new look. Avoid single-use plastic, reuse decorations and segregate waste for recycling.

## What else was suggested

Hold community activities such as tree plantation drives, neighbourhood clean-ups and environment awareness walks. Donate old clothes and usable household items to people in need. For travel during the festival, walk, cycle or use public and shared transport.

## How to check the air

Check the daily Air Quality Index (AQI) on CPCB's 'SAMEER' app and avoid going out when the air is bad. People are also encouraged to take part in WWF-India's 'Carbon Footprint Survey' to understand the impact of their actions on the environment.

## What institutions can do

Institutions and groups taking part can share details of their activity through an online form. This should include the organisation's name and location, activity details and date, the number of participants who took the pledge, geo-tagged photos and a brief report. Selected entries may be displayed on the EISCP website and social media platforms.
