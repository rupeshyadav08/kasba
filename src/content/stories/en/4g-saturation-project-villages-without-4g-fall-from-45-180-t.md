## What has happened

The Department of Telecom said on 11 November 2026 that the number of villages without 4G connectivity has fallen by about 75 per cent in four years. In 2022 there were 45,180 such villages, and in 2026 the number is 11,372.

According to the department, this is a big step in expanding digital connectivity in rural India.

## What the '4G saturation' project is

The project was made to bring 4G to 24,680 villages that had no connectivity earlier. The aim was that people in remote and low-facility areas could also use reliable mobile services and digital facilities.

Private telecom companies have also contributed by expanding their networks in rural areas.

## How much BSNL sites are used

Under the project, BSNL has started 18,291 sites, which are carrying a lot of data traffic in rural areas. In October 2026, these sites had a maximum of 10,21,048 active users in a day.

These users used more than 1.86 crore GB of data in total, which works out to an average of 18.3 GB per user per month. Each site serves about 56 active users on average.

## What happens next

The government has approved 26,000 more mobile network sites in the next phase to cover the remaining gaps. The department says difficult terrain, high infrastructure cost, scattered settlements and remote tribal areas remain big challenges for reaching connectivity.

Going ahead, the department will give priority to remote, tribal and aspirational areas. This will be done by setting up more sites, expanding network sharing and using existing infrastructure better.

## What this means for ordinary people

The direct benefit is for people in rural areas where the network is weak or absent. The department says this will help villages use digital governance, education, health services and financial services.

Note that this information gives no application, form or deadline for ordinary people. More details are available on the Department of Telecom's social media handles.
