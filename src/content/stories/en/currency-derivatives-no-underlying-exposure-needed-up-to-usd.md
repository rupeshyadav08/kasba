## What has changed

In a circular dated 5 November 2026, SEBI has changed one condition related to currency derivatives. Domestic clients and Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPIs) may now take long or short positions in currency derivatives contracts involving the Indian Rupee without having to establish that they have an underlying exposure. This is allowed up to a single limit of USD 5 million equivalent notional value.

The limit applies across all currency pairs involving INR and across all recognised stock exchanges put together. In other words, an investor's positions on all exchanges are counted together against this limit.

## Separate conditions for FPIs

FPIs must also ensure that their short positions across all contracts in FCY-INR pairs at all stock exchanges do not exceed USD 5 million.

If an FPI wants to take a long position above USD 5 million, it must have an underlying exposure in Indian debt or equity securities. This includes units of equity or debt mutual funds.

## What applies to domestic clients

Domestic clients may take positions above USD 5 million, but they must follow the conditions in RBI's Master Direction - Risk Management and Inter-Bank Dealings dated 5 July 2016 and RBI's circular dated 10 October 2026.

## Why this was done

RBI, in its A.P. (DIR Series) Circular No. 25 dated 10 October 2026, revised the threshold limits for taking positions in foreign exchange derivatives involving the Indian Rupee without establishing underlying exposure. RBI also prescribed additional requirements relating to cancellation and rebooking of foreign exchange derivative contracts and documentation for hedging contracted exposures.

SEBI has modified the relevant clause of its Master Circular for Stock Exchanges and Clearing Corporations dated 30 December 2024 to match this. All other requirements remain unchanged.

## What happens next

SEBI has directed stock exchanges and clearing corporations to put in place systems to implement the circular, including necessary amendments to their bye-laws, rules and regulations. They must bring the provisions to the notice of stock brokers and clearing members and disseminate them on their websites. They must also communicate the status of implementation to SEBI.

## Impact on ordinary people

This rule is for people and firms that trade currency derivatives on stock exchanges, and for foreign investors. If you are an ordinary investor and do not trade currency derivatives, it does not affect you directly. Those who do trade can check the new limit with their broker or on the exchange website.
