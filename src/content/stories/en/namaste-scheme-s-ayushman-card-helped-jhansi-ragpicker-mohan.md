## What happened

In a release dated 27 October 2026, the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment said that Mohan Lal (name changed), a 65-year-old ragpicker from Jhansi in Uttar Pradesh, received treatment for a heart condition with the help of a government scheme. He had received an Ayushman card under Ayushman Bharat-Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (PM-JAY) after being registered under the ragpicker component of the Namaste scheme.

## The treatment

According to the release, Mohan Lal worked for much of his life as a contract sanitation worker and ragpicker. He earned only Rs 101 to Rs 250 a day, which barely covered his family's basic needs. He was then found to have a serious blockage in his heart, and the cost of treatment became a major worry for him and his family.

With the Ayushman card, his treatment became possible at the All India Institute of Medical Sciences (AIIMS) in New Delhi on 8 May 2026. The release says the full cost of the treatment was borne under the scheme.

## What changed after treatment

After successful treatment, Mohan Lal recovered and returned to work to support his family. The release says access to health services reduced the financial burden of emergency medical care and gave him a chance to focus on improving his health.

## Who benefits

The news is relevant to ragpicker workers and other workers from weaker sections who are registered under the Namaste scheme and hold an Ayushman card. The release says such welfare schemes help remove barriers to essential treatment and protect people's dignity.

## Impact on ordinary people

People who are not registered under this scheme are not directly affected. This is not an announcement of a new scheme or a new deadline; it uses one worker's experience to show how the scheme helps.
