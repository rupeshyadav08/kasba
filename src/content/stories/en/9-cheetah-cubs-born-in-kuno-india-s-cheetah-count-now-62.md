## What has happened

Two new litters of cheetah cubs have been born at Kuno National Park in Sheopur district of Madhya Pradesh. The first birth came on 5 October 2026, when KJP 21, a 32-month-old female cheetah born in India, gave birth to five healthy cubs. The second came on 20 November 2026, when KOMG 3, a 30-month-old female brought from Botswana, gave birth to four healthy cubs.

Together the two births have added nine cubs to India's cheetah population. The country now has 62 cheetahs in all, of which 43 were born on Indian soil. India-born cheetahs now make up about 70 per cent of the total population.

## Why these births matter

With the birth of her cubs, KJP 21 became the third India-born female cheetah to breed, after Mukhi and KGP 12. The father of her cubs is a male cheetah brought from Botswana, so the Botswana genetic line has now entered the India-born population.

KOMG 3's litter is notable because she is the first female cheetah brought from Botswana to give birth in India. She delivered her cubs in the open natural setting after spending more than four months in the wild. This is also the second confirmed case of a cheetah breeding in the open forest since the programme began in 2022.

## The story of the cheetah's return

The cheetah was officially declared extinct in India in 1952. The last confirmed sighting was in 1947 in the Sal forests of present-day Koriya district in Chhattisgarh, where the last three wild Asiatic cheetahs were shot in December 1947. Heavy hunting, poaching and use of cheetahs for sport, along with loss of habitat to farming and a fall in prey animals, pushed the species out.

Project Cheetah began on 17 September 2022, when eight cheetahs brought from Namibia were released at Kuno. In February 2023, 12 more cheetahs arrived from South Africa. On 28 February 2026, nine more cheetahs from Botswana, six females and three males, reached Kuno. Bringing cheetahs from different countries has helped widen the genetic diversity of the population.

## Who benefits

Local communities around Kuno are active partners in the programme. More than 450 cheetah mitras in 80 villages around the park spread awareness and support conservation work. The programme has given direct employment to about 380 people, including trackers, patrol members and trained guides.

For general readers, this news carries no application, payment or deadline. It is an update on the progress of a wildlife conservation programme, run by the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change through the National Tiger Conservation Authority (NTCA).

## What will be watched next

Births alone do not prove that the cheetah population can sustain itself over the long term. The national action plan treats the survival of cubs beyond one year as an important milestone. How many of these cubs survive and go on to breed will therefore be key signs of the programme's progress.
