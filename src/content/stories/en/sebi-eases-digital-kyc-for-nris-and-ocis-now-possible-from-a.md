## What has changed

In a circular dated 4 November 2026, SEBI has revised the KYC process. Under the earlier rules, an intermediary had to ensure that the client's physical location was in India during digital KYC. This effectively stopped clients outside India from submitting KYC documents digitally.

Now, for an individual PROI client located in a FATF compliant country, the intermediary may accept digital submission of KYC information and supporting documents. SEBI said the change follows a review and representations from market participants.

## Who benefits

An individual PROI means an NRI, an OCI cardholder or a foreign national — someone who is not resident in India but wants an account-based relationship through a SEBI-registered intermediary.

The circular also says all KYC records shall be portable, meaning they can be shared with another intermediary. A KRA will tag individual attributes as "validated" if they are verified with an official or source database.

## What the KYC will involve

The intermediary will seek the client's PAN and CKYC ID, if available, and retrieve records from the KRA and Central KYC Records Registry. PAN will be verified with the Income Tax Database without insisting on the original or a copy. A passport copy and, where applicable, an OCI card will be collected.

For proof of identity and address, the client can give one of the specified officially valid documents. For an individual PROI, the current address is treated as an overseas address. If a foreign national's document does not show an address, a document from a foreign government department or a letter from a foreign embassy or mission in India can be accepted.

## Rules for video verification

Where physical in-person verification is not feasible, the intermediary will conduct Video In Person Verification. The intermediary's authorised official must be present during VIPV, and the process will be subject to concurrent audit. A specimen signature submitted digitally will be matched during VIPV.

## What to do next

NRIs, OCI cardholders and foreign nationals who want to open an investment account in India can approach their SEBI-registered intermediary and indicate that they live in a FATF compliant country. The full circular is available on SEBI's website.
