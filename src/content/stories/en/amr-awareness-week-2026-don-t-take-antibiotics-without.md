## What happened

The Ministry of Health and Family Welfare launched the national observance of World Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR) Awareness Week 2026 at Kartavya Bhavan in New Delhi on 18 November 2026. Union Health Secretary Punya Salila Srivastava addressed the event virtually.

World AMR Awareness Week is a global campaign observed every year from 18 to 24 November. It aims to raise awareness and understanding of AMR and to promote better practices among the public, One Health stakeholders and policymakers.

## What AMR is and why it worries officials

AMR means medicines gradually losing their effect. Srivastava said drugs that saved countless lives are losing their impact because of improper and excessive use. She warned that if unchecked, AMR could weaken decades of progress in medicine and make even common infections hard to treat.

This year's theme is 'One Health, One Action: Prevent AMR Now'. The One Health approach recognises how human health, animal health, plants and food systems, and the environment are linked. The Secretary said improper use of antimicrobials in all these areas helps resistance arise and spread.

## What the government is doing

Srivastava said the National Action Plan on AMR 2.0 (NAP AMR 2.0), 2025-2029 was launched by Union Health Minister J. P. Nadda in November 2025 during World AMR Awareness Week. It provides a broad framework for surveillance, infection prevention, responsible use of antimicrobials, research and coordinated action across sectors.

The National Programme on AMR Containment, run with the National Centre for Disease Control (NCDC), focuses on strengthening laboratories for AMR surveillance, infection prevention and control, prudent use of antimicrobials and awareness.

## What people were asked to do

The Secretary appealed to the public to act responsibly. She advised against taking medicines without a doctor's advice, sharing antibiotics or using leftover medicines. She reminded citizens that antibiotics do not work against viral infections such as the common cold and influenza.

She stressed cleanliness and hygiene, safe water, safe food and vaccination to prevent infection. This lowers the risk of infection and the need for antibiotics.

## What was said for doctors and medicine shops

Srivastava said antibiotics should be prescribed only when medically necessary and as per proper treatment guidelines. Pharmacists, she said, must dispense the right antimicrobials only on a valid prescription and tell patients how to use them correctly.

Stakeholder representatives noted that more than half of antimicrobial sales in India happen without a medical prescription, while about half of antibiotic prescriptions in outpatient departments are inappropriate. Measures suggested included public awareness campaigns, curbing prescribing and taking antibiotics without advice, proper disposal of antibiotics and expired medicines, displaying legal warnings at medicine shops against over-the-counter sale of antimicrobials, stopping the production of substandard and fake drugs, promoting proper antibiotic use in veterinary care, especially for food-producing animals, and maintaining quality standards in health facilities.

Senior officials of NHSRC, CDSCO, the Department of Animal Husbandry, the Indian Medical Association and the All India Association of Druggists and Chemists took part. Senior officials of states and union territories joined virtually.
