The Jute Commissioner, under the Ministry of Textiles, issued an order on 27 November 2026 fixing the maximum quantity of raw jute any person may hold at any time, whether in their own account or in a third party's account. The order has been issued under clauses 5 and 6(1)(iii) of the Jute and Jute Textiles Control Order, 2016.

## Why the limits were imposed

According to the Jute Commissioner, stock limits became necessary to improve the availability of raw jute for stakeholders. The order says price fluctuations and speculative increases can threaten the jute industry and may disrupt production and employment in it. The measures are meant to stabilise jute supply, prevent market manipulation and protect the interests of farmers, manufacturers and consumers across the country.

## Who can hold how much

Raw jute balers who have a baling press on the same premises, are registered with the Jute Commissioner's office and file stock returns on the Jute Smart portal (or had applied for registration by the date of the notification) may hold a maximum of 1,000 quintals at any time.

All other registered stockists may hold a maximum of 100 quintals. Stockists or balers who are not registered by the date of the notification and have not applied for registration, and registered stockists or balers who are not filing stock returns on the Jute Smart portal, may hold a maximum of 5 quintals.

Jute mills and manufacturing units that make jute goods from raw jute may hold raw jute equal to a maximum of 90 days of consumption at their current production rate.

## What holders must do now

Entities holding more than the fixed limit must sell the excess quantity and physically hand it over to the buyer on or before 10 December 2026. With immediate effect, all purchases of new stock must stop until the stock held comes within the prescribed limit.

Everyone covered by the order must declare and update their stock through returns on the Jute Smart portal (https://smart.jutecomm.gov.in) every fortnight. Raw jute kept in the same premises will be treated as a single stock, even if it stands in the names of different traders, stockists or balers. Where entities share a warehouse or baling press, the total quantity in that premises must not exceed the prescribed limit. Where a contract for the sale of raw jute has been made, the jute will be treated as held by the seller until it is physically handed over to the buyer.

## Checks and penalties

Authorised representatives of the Jute Commissioner and state police officers may enter and search premises, including warehouses, inspect books and records to verify that stock has been declared correctly, and seize raw jute where there is reasonable suspicion that it is being stored in violation of the order. State governments will be requested to help with enforcement action against entities hoarding raw jute.

The order states that any violation is punishable under clause 11 of the Jute and Jute Textiles Control Order, 2016, read with section 7 of the Essential Commodities Act, 1955, which provides for imprisonment and fine. Provisions for confiscation on violation are set out in section 6 of the Essential Commodities Act, 1955, and penalties for false statements in section 9.
